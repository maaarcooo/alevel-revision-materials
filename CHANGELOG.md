## 2026-10-19: Physics topic 8 and Turning Points added and checked

New decks for topic 8 (8.1 to 8.4, Nuclear physics) and for the Turning Points in Physics
option (12.1 to 12.3, specification section 3.12, examined in Paper 3BD). They were
generated, then checked against the specification and the A-level papers from 2017 to
2025: Paper 2 and Paper 3A for topic 8, and every Turning Points section of Paper 3BD,
each with its examiner report. Physics now has 47 decks and 2772 cards. Topic 8 and
Turning Points have no notes yet.

**Facts corrected**

- 12.2: the speed of light in water was measured, and found slower than in air, by
  Foucault. The cards credited Fizeau (2025 Paper 3BD Q03).
- 12.2: Hertz's measured speed of radio waves confirmed Maxwell's prediction for the
  speed of electromagnetic waves. The card said it showed radio waves are electromagnetic,
  which the 2025 examiner report says gets no credit.
- 12.2: Planck proposed that radiation is emitted in quanta, with energy proportional to
  frequency. The card said "discrete packets", and 2020 Paper 3BD Q02.3 does not condone
  "packet" for quanta.
- 12.2: an electromagnetic wave's electric and magnetic fields oscillate in phase. The
  card left out "in phase" (2019 Paper 3BD Q02.3).
- 12.2: the classical prediction for black-body radiation agreed with experiment at long
  wavelengths, and experiment shows intensity falling to zero at short wavelengths
  (2025 Paper 3BD Q02.1).
- 12.1: cathode rays come from the cathode when positive ions strike it. The card said
  only that electrons are "also emitted" from it (2017 and 2020 Paper 3BD Q01).
- 8.4: the coolant carries thermal energy to the heat exchanger. The card said only that
  it removes heat from the core (2024 Paper 2 Q04.1). Reactor shielding absorbs neutrons
  as well as gamma radiation, and is steel and concrete rather than lead.
- 8.4: other coolants are now carbon dioxide gas and liquid sodium, the ones the papers
  use, in place of molten salt and helium.
- 8.2: the half-life is written $T_{1/2}$, as on the data and formulae sheet.

**Added from the mark schemes**

- 8.1: testing data against the inverse-square law by checking $Cx^2$ is constant at three
  or more distances.
- 8.2: modelling decay with dice, where $\lambda = \frac{1}{n}$ per throw.
- 8.3: the X-rays and gamma rays emitted after electron capture, technetium-99m emitting
  only gamma rays, and the closest-approach distance depending on the alpha particle's
  energy.
- 8.4: $E = mc^2$ for any energy change (a stretched spring), a worked D-T fusion energy,
  why fission fragments decay by $\beta^-$, the properties of a coolant, U-238 not
  undergoing fission, and why a moderator needs light nuclei.
- 12.1: Crookes' paddle wheel, the two pds in an electron gun, the fine beam tube's gas,
  $\frac{e}{m} = \frac{v^2}{2V_A}$ and $\frac{e}{m} = \frac{ad}{V}$, the forces on a
  rising oil drop and the assumptions in finding its radius.
- 12.2: corpuscles' straight-line paths, Fizeau's next dark frequency $3f_0$ and what his
  result implied, the classical predictions for the photoelectric effect, and electron
  diffraction as support for de Broglie's hypothesis.
- 12.3: the size of the expected and detectable fringe shift, why fringes appear in
  every orientation, why special relativity does not apply to an accelerating
  spacecraft, applying the second postulate, the muon measurements, and why
  $\frac{1}{2}mv^2$ with relativistic mass is wrong.

**Removed**

- Cards that repeat a definition another deck owns: alpha and beta particles, the strong
  force's range, electron capture, ionisation, de Broglie's equation, the photoelectric
  observations, electron diffraction rings, specific charge, $F = BQv$, $r = \frac{mv}{BQ}$,
  $W = QV$, moles and molar mass, atomic and nuclear sizes, and mass-energy equivalence
  in 12.3.
- Trivia and figures beyond the specification: Dalton's and Bohr's models, the
  quantum-mechanical model, potassium-40 in bananas, half-lives that questions supply,
  the formation of carbon-14, and the costs of commissioning a power station.
- Fill-in-the-blank cards, rewritten as questions.

## 2026-10-16: Physics topics 1 to 5 checked against A-level papers

Topics 1 to 5 had their mark-scheme pass against the AS papers on 5 October. The A-level
Papers 1 and 3A from 2017 to 2025 examine them too, so they had the pass again. Most
cards already said what the mark schemes credit. Physics now has 2222 cards, 29 more,
most of them on the practical and graph skills that Paper 3A tests every year.

**Facts corrected**

- 4.6: $P = Fv$ is not limited to constant velocity. It gives the power at the instant
  the speed is $v$, including while an object accelerates, as 2023 Paper 1 Q02.3 uses
  it. The card and note had limited it to constant velocity.
- 4.3: in the two light gate method for $g$, residual magnetism in the electromagnet does
  not lengthen the timed fall, because the timer starts at the first gate. The card and
  note gave it as the systematic error. Air resistance replaced it, and a new card
  explains why the release delay is not timed. Standard physics, not from a mark scheme.
- 4.8: a vernier scale reduces the uncertainty in each extension reading through its
  finer resolution. The card and note said it reduces systematic error. Standard physics.
- 2.1: the neutrino was proposed because beta particles have a range of kinetic energies
  while each decay releases a fixed amount of energy. The card gave only the range and
  conservation of energy, which 2022 Paper 1 Q02.2 says is not enough.
- 5.4 note: an example called 0.7 A two significant figures and rounded the lost volts
  to 0.2 V. It now gives 0.74 A and 0.22 V. Arithmetic.

**Reworded to the wording that earns the mark**

- 1.2 note: a measuring cylinder is read with the eye level with the bottom of the
  meniscus. "At eye level" alone is not enough (2018 Paper 3A Q03.3).
- 3.1: transverse and longitudinal waves are defined by the direction of energy
  transfer. 2025 Paper 1 Q02.1 does not allow "direction of travel" or "vibrations", so
  the definitions, their reverse cards and the demonstration cards changed.
- 3.2: a stationary wave forms when a wave is reflected and the incident and reflected
  waves, travelling in opposite directions, superpose. 2018 Paper 1 Q03.1 gives no credit
  for "interferes with itself". The control variables in the stationary waves practical
  are now both quantities, tension and mass per unit length (2025 Paper 3A Q01.1).
- 4.3 and 4.6: energy dissipated by drag or friction heats something that must be named,
  such as the air or the object. 2018 Paper 1 Q01.3 gave no credit for "heat and sound".
- 5.1: each coulomb gains energy equal to the cell's EMF, not its "voltage" (2023 Paper 1
  Q03.1).
- 5.2: the dependent variable in the resistivity practical is the resistance, from the
  voltmeter and ammeter readings, and the PD across the wire is no longer listed as a
  control variable. Standard practice for required practical 5, which 2024 Paper 3A
  Q01.6 analyses as resistance against length.
- 5.3: cells in series give the sum of their EMFs, not of their PDs.
- 5.4: the voltmeter in the EMF practical reads the terminal PD across the whole external
  circuit (2020 Paper 3A Q03.1).

**Added**

- 1.1: an order of magnitude is given as the power of ten alone (2018 and 2020 Paper 3A).
- 1.2: reading a meniscus and using a mirror to avoid parallax (2018, 2019, 2021 and 2022
  Paper 3A), labelling axes with a solidus, finding an intercept when an axis has a false
  origin, testing data for direct and inverse proportion and judging the difference
  (2021 and 2022 Paper 3A), and log graphs for exponential and power laws with their axis
  labels (2017, 2018 and 2025 Paper 3A). The 1.2 note has a new section on testing
  relationships.
- 2.3: the quark change in electron capture (2025 Paper 1 Q01.1).
- 2.4: a smaller work function gives a larger stopping potential (2020 Paper 1 Q02.5).
- 3.1: showing that microwaves are polarised by rotating the receiver (2021 Paper 1
  Q02.1).
- 3.4: the maxima for light with a range of wavelengths (2019 Paper 1 Q03.4), and a use
  of a grating outside astronomy (2024 Paper 1 Q03.3).
- 3.5: light escaping from a sharply bent optical fibre (2021 Paper 1 Q03.3).
- 4.2: three forces in equilibrium act through one point (2017 Paper 1 Q05.2), and the
  assumption that a ruler is uniform (2018 Paper 3A Q01.1).
- 4.3: the acceleration at an instant from a tangent to a curved velocity-time graph
  (2018 Paper 1 Q01.1).
- 4.5: a longer stopping distance reduces the force, by work done (2019 Paper 1 Q05.5,
  2024 Paper 1 Q05.3), and the lighter fragment of an explosion gains more kinetic energy
  (2024 Paper 1 Q04.1).
- 5.2: keeping superconducting cables below the critical temperature (2020 Paper 1
  Q05.5).
- 5.3: using meters to show conservation of charge and energy (2024 Paper 1 Q06.4).
- 5.4: the EMF and internal resistance of cells in series and in parallel (2019 Paper 1
  Q07.2), and why a lamp dims when another component is connected in parallel with it
  (2022 Paper 1 Q04.5).

Each change went into the matching note as well. The 5.3 and 5.4 notes now write EMF in
capitals, not "e.m.f".

**Left as they were**

- 2.2, 2.5, 3.3, 4.1, 4.4 and 4.7 needed no change: their definitions and explanations

- Paper 2 was not used, since it examines sections 6.2 to 8. Multiple-choice questions

## 2026-10-16: Revision notes for Physics topics 6 and 7

Every Physics subtopic now has a note: 16 new notes for 6.1 to 6.6 and 7.1 to 7.10,
written from the Save My Exams and PMT source notes with the `revision-notes-generator`
skill. Each follows the AQA 7408 specification section named on its first line, matches
the facts in its deck, and carries the wording the A-level mark schemes credit (internal
energy as a sum, absolute zero as zero kinetic energy, resonance as maximum amplitude).
The comparison of gravitational and electric fields (specification 3.7.1) is in 7.4.
No deck changed. Physics now has 40 notes.

**Where the notes differ from the source notes**

- 6.2: the period of liquid in a U-tube is $2\pi\sqrt{\frac{L}{2g}}$ for a column of
  total length $L$. The source used $2\pi\sqrt{\frac{l}{g}}$ with $l$ the column length.
- 6.4: the internal energy of a system rises by heating it or doing work on it. One
  source listed "increase the temperature" as a separate way, which is a result, not a
  cause. The source claim that metals conduct heat well because of a low specific heat
  capacity was left out.
- 6.4: a definition of internal energy needs "sum" or "total", as the 2019 mark scheme
  shows. One source said the word "random" was the one that earns the mark.
- 6.5: pressure is force per unit area, from the rate of change of momentum of the
  molecules. One source defined it as the frequency of collisions per unit area.
- 6.5: at absolute zero the volume or pressure of a gas extrapolates to zero. One source
  said they are zero. Molar mass is given in $\text{kg mol}^{-1}$, not grams.
- 6.3: the source definition of forced vibrations said the amplitude is large at low
  driving frequencies. It was left out, since the amplitude is greatest near the natural
  frequency.
- 7.7: the time for a capacitor to discharge does not depend on the initial current. One
  source said a larger initial current makes it take longer. The source also listed the
  current as a control variable in required practical 9, which it cannot be.
- 7.9: a magnet falling through a coil is slowed by magnetic forces from the induced
  current. One source called them electrostatic.

## 2026-10-10: Physics 6.1 to 6.3 added

Three new decks complete topic 6: 6.1 Circular Motion (40 cards), 6.2 Simple Harmonic
Motion (71) and 6.3 Forced Vibrations & Resonance (35). They were written from the
source notes for those sections against AQA 7408 section 3.6.1, to the current card
rules (flashcard-v3.10). Physics now has 2185 cards in 40 decks. They have no revision
notes yet, and no mark-scheme pass.

**Where the decks differ from the source notes**

- Fixed: a freely vibrating system oscillates at its natural frequency. The source
  called it the resonant frequency.
- Fixed: a centripetal force does no work because it is perpendicular to the motion.
  The source gave only that the kinetic energy does not change.
- Fixed: timing ten or more oscillations reduces the percentage uncertainty in the
  period. The source said it reduces random errors.
- Tightened: angular speed is the same for every point on one rigid rotating object,
  and linear speed is proportional to the distance from the axis. The source said an
  object further from the centre has a smaller angular velocity, which is only true at
  the same linear speed.
- Skipped: the period of liquid in a U-tube. The source's formula takes the wrong
  length, and the specification says questions on other oscillators give the
  information needed.
- Skipped: the source's list of everyday examples of simple harmonic motion, several of
  which are not simple harmonic.
- Added beyond the source: the forces on a mass at the top and bottom of a vertical
  circle, the phase of a driven system relative to its driver, the total energy
  $\frac{1}{2}m\omega^2 A^2$, and the fiducial marker, all of which exam questions use.
- Kept: $x = A\sin\omega t$ for an oscillator timed from its equilibrium position,
  although the specification names only $x = A\cos\omega t$.

## Provenance

All content is AI-generated.

- **2025 to mid 2026.** Decks and notes were generated from Physics & Maths Tutor (PMT)
  and Save My Exams (SME) notes, first with hand-written prompts (versions 1 and 2) and
  then with the `flashcard-generator` and `revision-notes-generator` skills (versions
  3.2 to 3.8), using Claude Sonnet 4.5, Opus 4.5 and Opus 4.6. The full record of which
  model and skill produced which version is `VERSIONS.md` at the `pre-baseline` tag.
- **October 2026.** The baseline above was produced with Claude Opus 5.5 in Claude
  Code, by merging those versions and checking them against the source notes and the
  OCR H446 and AQA 7408 specifications.
