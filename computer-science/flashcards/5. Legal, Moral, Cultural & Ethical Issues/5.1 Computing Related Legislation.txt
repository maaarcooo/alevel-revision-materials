What is the Data Protection Act 1998? | A law that protects personal data from being misused, applying to data stored on computers and in organised paper filing systems
A law that protects personal data from being misused — what act is this? | The Data Protection Act 1998
What is personal data under the DPA? | Any data that can be used to identify a living person, including data that can identify someone when combined with other data
Any data that can be used to identify a living person — what term is this? | Personal data
What is a data subject? | An individual who can be identified by personal data
An individual who can be identified by personal data — what term is this? | Data subject
How many Data Protection Principles does the DPA contain? | Eight
State DPA Principle 1 | Personal data shall be processed fairly and lawfully
State DPA Principle 2 | Personal data shall be obtained only for one or more specified and lawful purposes
State DPA Principle 3 | Personal data shall be adequate, relevant, and not excessive for its purpose
State DPA Principle 4 | Personal data shall be accurate and where necessary kept up to date
State DPA Principle 5 | Personal data shall not be kept for longer than is necessary for its purpose
State DPA Principle 6 | Personal data shall be processed in accordance with the rights of data subjects under the Act
State DPA Principle 7 | Appropriate technical and organisational measures shall be taken against unauthorised or unlawful processing and against accidental loss, destruction, or damage of personal data
State DPA Principle 8 | Personal data shall not be transferred outside the European Economic Area unless the destination country ensures an adequate level of data protection
Name the three exemptions from the Data Protection Act | Domestic purposes, law enforcement, intelligence services processing
Under the DPA, what is the domestic purposes exemption? | If personal data is used only for personal activities such as writing to friends or taking photos for enjoyment, the DPA does not apply
What is a Data Controller? | A member of staff appointed by a company to ensure the Data Protection Principles are not breached and to communicate with the Information Commissioner
What is a Subject Access Request (SAR)? | A request by a data subject for a copy of all the personal data a company holds on them
A request by a data subject for a copy of all personal data held on them — what is this called? | Subject Access Request (SAR)
What must a company do before responding to a Subject Access Request? | Verify the identity of the data subject, then send the data copy securely
What is the right to data portability under data protection law? | The right of a data subject to obtain and reuse their personal data across different services
What is the right to be forgotten under data protection law? | The right of a data subject to have their personal data deleted in certain circumstances
What right do data subjects have regarding automated decisions? | The right to challenge automated decision making and profiling
What penalty can companies face for breaching the Data Protection Act? | Extremely large fines
What legislation replaced the Data Protection Act 1998? | The General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and the Data Protection Act 2018
What is the Computer Misuse Act 1990? | A law concerning the malicious use of computers, originally created to ensure computer hacking was covered by law
A law concerning the malicious use of computers — what act is this? | The Computer Misuse Act 1990
What is offence 1 under the Computer Misuse Act? | Unauthorised access to computer materials
What is offence 2 under the Computer Misuse Act? | Unauthorised access with intent to commit further offences
What is offence 3 under the Computer Misuse Act? | Unauthorised modification of computer material
How do CMA offences 2 and 3 differ? | Offence 2 is accessing a system without authorisation intending to commit further offences (e.g. accessing a markbook to change grades); offence 3 is actually carrying out the unauthorised modification
How do penalties compare across the three CMA offences? | They become progressively more severe from offence 1 to 3, with each punishable by imprisonment
What is the Copyright, Designs and Patents Act 1988? | A law that protects the intellectual property of individuals and companies, making it illegal to copy, modify, or distribute work without permission
A law protecting intellectual property and making it illegal to copy, modify, or distribute work without permission — what act is this? | The Copyright, Designs and Patents Act 1988
When does copyright apply to an original work under the CDPA? | Automatically from the moment of creation
How long does copyright last under the CDPA? | 25 to 70 years after the death of the creator, depending on the type of work
Who is responsible for taking action if work is copied under the CDPA? | The individual whose work has been copied
How were computer programs affected by the 1992 amendment to the CDPA? | They were classified as a type of literary work, meaning copyright automatically applies to code
What are primary breaches of the CDPA? | Acts that directly infringe copyright: copying an original work, issuing copies to the public, renting or lending copies, performing or showing the work in public, or making an adaptation
What are secondary breaches of the CDPA? | Acts that facilitate infringement: importing copies, possessing or dealing with copies, providing means to make copies, permitting premises to be used for copying, or providing equipment for a performance of a copy
What is the Regulation of Investigatory Powers Act 2000? | A law that covers investigation, surveillance, and interception of communication by public bodies
A law covering investigation, surveillance, and interception of communication by public bodies — what act is this? | The Regulation of Investigatory Powers Act 2000 (RIPA)
What three activities does RIPA regulate? | Investigation, surveillance, and interception of communication
What condition must be met before a public body can intercept online communications under RIPA? | A warrant must be issued
What does RIPA require of ISPs and mobile phone companies? | To give up information on request from an authorised authority and to have sufficient hardware installed on their networks to facilitate surveillance
Why is RIPA considered controversial? | Its powers extend to small agencies like local councils, and some people view it as an invasion of privacy or believe it is used improperly
