What is thinking procedurally? | Breaking a problem down into smaller parts that are easier to understand and to design
Which two processes are applied to a problem before algorithms are created to solve it? | Abstraction to remove unimportant details, then decomposition into sub-problems
What are the components of a problem? | The smaller, simpler tasks and sub-procedures it can be broken down into, to be completed modularly
What is top-down design? | A method of decomposition that breaks a problem into major tasks, then into smaller sub-tasks, until each can be solved by a single subroutine or module
By what other name is top-down design known? | Stepwise refinement
In top-down design, what do higher levels show compared with lower levels? | Higher levels give an overview of the problem. Lower levels specify its components in detail
In top-down design, when should a sub-task stop being broken down? | When it cannot be broken down further, is easily solved and is clear, so a single subroutine or module can solve it
Why is top-down design preferred for very large problems? | Splitting the problem into levels makes it manageable and lets the tasks be shared between people
Why is decomposed code simpler to test? | Each subroutine is self-contained, so it can be tested on its own (unit testing) before being integrated
Why does decomposition make errors easier to find and fix? | Each subroutine is self-contained and well documented, so an error can be traced to one module
How does decomposition support code reuse? | A subroutine can be reused wherever it is needed instead of rewriting the code
How does decomposition help a team of developers? | Sub-problems can be shared out according to each person's skills and developed separately
How do the components of a solution differ from the components of a problem? | Components of the problem are the parts that must be solved. Components of the solution are how each part will be implemented
Which details about data are decided when identifying the components of a solution? | The data types of inputs and outputs, the data structures needed, and the format of the output
Which details about processing are decided when identifying the components of a solution? | The libraries required, the constructs and data transformations needed, and how efficient the algorithm must be
How is procedural abstraction used when thinking procedurally? | Each component is implemented as a procedure that carries out a sequence of instructions to achieve one goal
In thinking procedurally, what is meant by a procedure? | A unit of computation that performs a single task, not strictly a subroutine that returns no value
Why is it useful to look for existing modules, subroutines or libraries when designing a solution? | Reusing them reduces the complexity of development
Why must the order of steps be considered when combining subroutines into a solution? | Some subroutines need data from others, so they cannot run until that data exists
What must be done to inputs before they are passed on to other subroutines? | They must be validated
When can subroutines be executed simultaneously? | When they do not need data from each other
Why must the order of operations be written explicitly into a program? | An order that seems obvious to a person is not enforced unless it is coded, so operations could run in an order that raises an error
Give an example of an order of operations that a program must enforce | A food delivery app must not let food be chosen before the location is confirmed, or payment be made before the order is confirmed
What is a hierarchy chart? | A tree-structured diagram that shows how a problem is decomposed and how its modules and subroutines relate to each other
A tree-structured diagram that shows how a problem is decomposed and how its modules and subroutines relate to each other - what term is this? | Hierarchy chart
Why are diagrams preferred to tables and lists for decomposing larger problems? | Tables and lists quickly become cumbersome as the problem grows
