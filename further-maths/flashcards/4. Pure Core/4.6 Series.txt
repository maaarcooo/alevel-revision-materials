What is $\sum_{r=1}^{n} r$? | $\frac{1}{2}n(n + 1)$
What is $\sum_{r=1}^{n} r^2$? | $\frac{1}{6}n(n + 1)(2n + 1)$
What is $\sum_{r=1}^{n} r^3$? | $\frac{1}{4}n^2(n + 1)^2$
What is $\sum_{r=1}^{n} 1$? | $n$
What is $\sum_{r=1}^{10} r^2$? | $\frac{1}{6} \times 10 \times 11 \times 21 = 385$
How is a sum such as $\sum_{r=1}^{n} (3r^2 - 2r)$ found? | Split it, $3\sum r^2 - 2\sum r$, then use the standard formulae
How is $\sum_{r=k}^{n}$ found from sums that start at $r = 1$? | $\sum_{r=1}^{n} - \sum_{r=1}^{k-1}$
Why are common factors taken out before expanding when simplifying a sum? | Taking out factors such as $\frac{1}{6}n(n + 1)$ first keeps the algebra short and leaves the answer factorised
How is a sum of squares of odd numbers written with sigma notation? | As $\sum (2r - 1)^2$, with $r$ running from 1 to the number of terms
How many terms are in $1^2 + 3^2 + 5^2 + \ldots + 779^2$? | $2r - 1 = 779$ gives $r = 390$
How are the limits found for the sum of the cube numbers from 216 to 512000? | Take cube roots of the end values, giving $r = 6$ to $r = 80$
What is the method of differences? | Write each term as $f(r) - f(r + k)$, list the terms so that most cancel, and add the ones left at the start and the end
How is a term such as $\frac{1}{r(r + 2)}$ prepared for the method of differences? | Split it into partial fractions, $\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{r} - \frac{1}{r + 2}\right)$
What is $\sum_{r=1}^{n} \left(\frac{1}{r} - \frac{1}{r + 1}\right)$? | $1 - \frac{1}{n + 1}$
Which terms are left when $\frac{1}{r} - \frac{1}{r + 2}$ is summed from $r = 1$ to $n$? | The first two positive terms and the last two negative terms, $1 + \frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{n + 1} - \frac{1}{n + 2}$
How many terms must be written out in the method of differences? | Enough at the start and at the end to show the cancelling pattern and every term that is left
How is the sum to infinity found after the method of differences? | Find the sum to $n$ terms, then its limit as $n \to \infty$
How is the limit written when finding a sum to infinity? | With $\lim_{n \to \infty}$ and a statement such as $\frac{1}{n + 1} \to 0$, never substituting $\infty$ as a number
When does an infinite series have a sum? | When its sum to $n$ terms tends to a finite limit as $n \to \infty$
