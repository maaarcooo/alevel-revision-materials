What is a binary operation on a set? | A rule that combines any two elements of the set to give a single result
What does it mean for an operation $\circ$ to be associative? | $(a \circ b) \circ c = a \circ (b \circ c)$ for all $a$, $b$ and $c$
What does it mean for an operation $\circ$ to be commutative? | $a \circ b = b \circ a$ for all $a$ and $b$
How is an operation proved to be associative? | Expand both groupings for general elements and show they are equal, since a numerical example alone is not a proof
How is an operation shown not to be associative? | Give one example with $(a \circ b) \circ c \neq a \circ (b \circ c)$
Besides associativity, which three properties must a group have? | Closure, an identity element, and an inverse for every element
What does it mean for a set to be closed under $\circ$? | $a \circ b$ is in the set for all $a$ and $b$ in the set
How is closure shown from a Cayley table? | State that every entry in the table is an element of the set, not just "from the table"
How is closure shown to fail? | Find two elements of the set whose combination is not in the set, checking that both really are elements
Why is $\{1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}$ under multiplication modulo 6 not a group? | $2 \times_6 3 = 0$, which is not in the set
What is an identity element $e$? | An element with $e \circ a = a \circ e = a$ for every $a$
How is the identity of an operation such as $a \oplus b$ found when asked to determine it? | Solve $a \oplus e = a$ for $e$, rather than only checking a guessed value
What is an inverse of $a$? | An element $a^{-1}$ with $a \circ a^{-1} = a^{-1} \circ a = e$
How are inverses shown from a Cayley table? | State each inverse pair, not just that the identity appears in every row
What is $(ab)^{-1}$ in a group? | $b^{-1}a^{-1}$, in the reverse order
What is an abelian group? | A group whose operation is commutative
How does a Cayley table show that a group is abelian? | It is symmetric about the leading diagonal
How is it shown that a group is not abelian? | Give two specific elements with $ab \neq ba$
What is the Latin square property of a group table? | Each element appears exactly once in every row and every column
How is a Cayley table completed with the Latin square property? | Fill each cell where only one element is still missing from its row or column
What is the order of a group? | The number of elements in it
What is the order of an element $a$? | The smallest positive integer $n$ with $a^n = e$
What is the order of the identity? | 1
What is a self-inverse element? | An element that is its own inverse, so a non-identity one has order 2
How is the order of an element found? | Work out its successive powers until the identity appears
What is the order of 2 in the group $\{1, 2, 3, 4\}$ under multiplication modulo 5? | Its powers are 2, 4, 3, 1, so its order is 4
What must the order of an element of a finite group divide? | The group's order, by Lagrange's theorem
What is a subgroup? | A subset that is itself a group under the same operation
What is a proper subgroup? | A subgroup other than the whole group. Including or leaving out $\{e\}$ is not penalised
How is a subset shown to be a subgroup? | Show that it is closed and contains the identity and the inverse of each of its elements
What is Lagrange's theorem? | The order of a subgroup of a finite group divides the order of the group
What does Lagrange's theorem not say? | That every factor of a group's order is the order of a subgroup, which is false in general
What orders can proper subgroups of a group of order 10 have? | 1, 2 and 5
How does Lagrange's theorem show that a set of 4 elements cannot be a subgroup of a group of order $4n + 2$? | 4 does not divide $4n + 2 = 2(2n + 1)$
How is the subgroup generated by an element found? | List its successive powers until the identity appears
What is a cyclic group? | A group in which every element is a power of one element, a generator
How is a group shown to be cyclic? | Name a generator, or an element whose order equals the order of the group
How is a group shown not to be cyclic? | Give the order of every element and show none equals the order of the group
Why is every cyclic group abelian? | Any two elements are powers of a generator $g$, and $g^ag^b = g^{a+b} = g^bg^a$
Why is every group of prime order $p$ cyclic? | By Lagrange's theorem every non-identity element has order $p$, so it generates the group
Up to isomorphism, which orders up to 7 have just one group? | Every order except 4 and 6, and each of these groups is cyclic
What are the two groups of order 4? | The cyclic group $C_4$ and the Klein group $K_4$
How do $C_4$ and $K_4$ differ? | $C_4$ has an element of order 4, but every non-identity element of $K_4$ is self-inverse
What are the two groups of order 6? | The cyclic group $C_6$ and the non-abelian group $D_3$, the symmetries of an equilateral triangle
What is the order of the symmetry group of a regular $n$-gon? | $2n$, with $n$ rotations and $n$ reflections
Which set forms a group under multiplication modulo a prime $p$? | $\{1, 2, \ldots, p - 1\}$
What does it mean for two groups to be isomorphic? | Their elements can be paired one to one so that the operation is preserved, so they have the same structure
How is it shown that two groups are not isomorphic? | Find a structural difference and state it in full, such as the orders of their elements
How is it shown that two cyclic groups of the same order are isomorphic? | Name a generator of each, showing both are cyclic of that order
Why does every group of even order contain a self-inverse element? | Elements that are not self-inverse pair off with their inverses, which leaves an odd number of non-identity elements to be their own inverses
How is it proved that a group in which every element is self-inverse is abelian? | $ab$ is self-inverse, so $ab = (ab)^{-1}$, and reversing the order with each element self-inverse gives $ba$
How are elements combined in a group of pairs under addition modulo $m$ and modulo $n$? | Component by component, $(a, b) + (c, d) = (a +_m c, b +_n d)$
When is the group of pairs under addition modulo $m$ and modulo $n$ cyclic? | When $\mathrm{hcf}(m, n) = 1$, with $(1, 1)$ as a generator
