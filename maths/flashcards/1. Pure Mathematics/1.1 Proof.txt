What is proof by deduction? | Starting from known facts or assumptions and reaching the conclusion through a series of logical steps
Starting from known facts or assumptions and reaching the conclusion through a series of logical steps - what term is this? | Proof by deduction
What is proof by exhaustion? | Splitting the statement into a finite number of cases that cover every possibility, and proving each case
Splitting the statement into a finite number of cases that cover every possibility, and proving each case - what term is this? | Proof by exhaustion
What is disproof by counter example? | Finding one value for which the condition of the statement is true but its conclusion is false
Finding one value for which the condition of the statement is true but its conclusion is false - what term is this? | Disproof by counter example
What is proof by contradiction? | Assuming the statement is false and showing that this leads to a contradiction
Assuming the statement is false and showing that this leads to a contradiction - what term is this? | Proof by contradiction
What does $P \Rightarrow Q$ mean? | If $P$ is true then $Q$ is true ($P$ implies $Q$)
What does $P \Leftrightarrow Q$ mean? | $P$ is true if and only if $Q$ is true, so each statement implies the other
What does the symbol $\equiv$ mean? | Is identically equal to, so true for every value of the variable
What is a rational number? | A number that can be written as $\frac{a}{b}$, where $a$ and $b$ are integers and $b \neq 0$
What is an irrational number? | A real number that cannot be written as a fraction of two integers
Why is a recurring decimal such as $0.333\ldots$ rational? | It can be written as a fraction of two integers, here $\frac{1}{3}$
How are a general even number and a general odd number written? | $2n$ and $2n + 1$, where $n$ is an integer
How are two different odd numbers written in a general proof? | With different letters, such as $2m + 1$ and $2n + 1$, where $m$ and $n$ are integers
How are the integers split into cases when a proof concerns division by 3? | $3k$, $3k + 1$ and $3k + 2$, where $k$ is an integer
Why do numerical examples not prove a general statement? | They show only those cases, not every possible value
What must the last line of a proof contain? | A conclusion stating that the result has been proved
How is it shown that an expression such as $4k + 2$ is not a multiple of 4? | State that it leaves a remainder of 2 when divided by 4
When a counter example disproves a statement about primes, what must be shown? | That the number is not prime, by giving a factor, such as $85 = 5 \times 17$
When proving that a product such as $(m - n)(m + n)$ is not prime, what must also be shown? | That neither factor equals 1
What is a counter example to "if $x$ and $y$ are irrational then $x + y$ is irrational"? | $\sqrt{2} + (-\sqrt{2}) = 0$, which is rational
What assumption starts the proof that $\sqrt{2}$ is irrational? | $\sqrt{2} = \frac{a}{b}$, where $a$ and $b$ are integers with no common factors and $b \neq 0$
In the proof that $\sqrt{2}$ is irrational, what follows from $a^2 = 2b^2$? | $a^2$ is even, so $a$ is even
In the proof that $\sqrt{2}$ is irrational, what follows from writing $a = 2k$? | $4k^2 = 2b^2$, so $b^2 = 2k^2$ and $b$ is even
What contradiction ends the proof that $\sqrt{2}$ is irrational? | $a$ and $b$ are both even, so they have a common factor of 2
What assumption starts the proof that there are infinitely many primes? | There is a finite list of all the primes, $p_1, p_2, \ldots, p_n$
Which number is considered in the proof that there are infinitely many primes? | $N = p_1 p_2 \ldots p_n + 1$
Why does $N = p_1 p_2 \ldots p_n + 1$ give a contradiction? | Dividing $N$ by any prime in the list leaves remainder 1, so $N$ is prime or has a prime factor that is not in the list
What must the end of a proof by contradiction state? | That the result contradicts the assumption, so the assumption is false and the original statement is true
