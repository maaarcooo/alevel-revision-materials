What is a population in statistics? | The whole set of items or people that are of interest
The whole set of items or people that are of interest - what term is this? | Population
What is a sample? | A subset of the population, from which data are collected
A subset of the population, from which data are collected - what term is this? | Sample
What is a census? | Data collected from every member of the population
Give two reasons for using a sample rather than a census | A sample is quicker and cheaper. It is the only option when testing destroys the items
How can a sample be used to make an informal inference about a population? | Use the sample statistic, such as the sample mean or proportion, as an estimate of the population value
Why can two random samples from the same population lead to different conclusions? | Each sample contains different members, so its statistics vary by chance from sample to sample
What is a simple random sample? | A sample in which every member of the population has an equal chance of being chosen, independently of the others
A sample in which every member of the population has an equal chance of being chosen, independently of the others - what term is this? | Simple random sample
How is a simple random sample chosen? | Number every member of the population, then use random numbers to select members, ignoring repeats
Unless told otherwise, what may be assumed about the population when taking a random sample? | It is large enough to sample without replacement
Why is a sample chosen by a method that makes some members more likely to be picked not random? | Each member does not have an equal chance of being chosen
What is the disadvantage of a small simple random sample from a population made up of distinct groups? | By chance it may include too many from one group and too few from another, so it does not represent the groups
What is opportunity sampling? | Choosing members of the population who are available and convenient at the time
What is the advantage of opportunity sampling? | It is quick and easy
What is the disadvantage of opportunity sampling? | It is unlikely to be representative of the population, so it may be biased
What is systematic sampling? | Choosing every $k$th member from an ordered list, starting from a randomly chosen member among the first $k$
What is the disadvantage of systematic sampling? | It can be biased if the list has a repeating pattern that matches the interval $k$
What is stratified sampling? | Dividing the population into groups (strata) by a characteristic, then taking a random sample from each group in proportion to its size
What is the advantage of stratified sampling over simple random sampling? | Each group in the population is represented in the right proportion
What is the disadvantage of stratified sampling? | The size of each group in the population must be known
A school has 600 students, 240 of them in the sixth form. How many sixth formers belong in a stratified sample of 50? | $50 \times \frac{240}{600} = 20$
What is cluster sampling? | Dividing the population into groups (clusters), choosing some clusters at random, and sampling the members of the chosen clusters
What is the advantage of cluster sampling? | It is cheaper and quicker when the population is spread over a wide area
What is the disadvantage of cluster sampling? | The sample may not be representative if the clusters differ from each other
What is quota sampling? | An interviewer chooses members until a set number (quota) from each group of the population has been filled
What is the advantage of quota sampling? | Each group is represented without needing a list of the whole population
What is the disadvantage of quota sampling? | It is not random, so the interviewer's choices can bias it
What is a biased sample? | A sample that does not represent the population, because some members or groups are more likely to be chosen than others
Why can results from a sample at one school not support conclusions about all students in the UK? | The sample is not representative of the whole population, since students elsewhere may differ
