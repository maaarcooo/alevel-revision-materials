What is uncertainty in a measurement? | An estimate of the difference between a measurement reading and the true value
An estimate of the difference between a measurement reading and the true value — what term is this? | Uncertainty
What are random errors? | Unpredictable fluctuations in readings caused by uncontrollable factors such as environmental conditions
Which property of measurements do random errors affect? | Precision — they cause a wider spread of results about the mean value
How can random errors be reduced? | Repeat measurements several times and calculate an average from them
What are systematic errors? | Errors arising from faulty instruments or flaws in the experimental method that are repeated consistently every time
Which property of measurements do systematic errors affect? | Accuracy — all readings are shifted by the same amount from the true value
How can systematic errors be reduced? | Recalibrate or replace the instrument, and make corrections or adjustments to the technique
What is a zero error? | A type of systematic error where an instrument gives a non-zero reading when the true value is zero
What is precision? | How close repeated measurements are to each other (how little spread there is about the mean value)
How close repeated measurements are to each other — what term is this? | Precision
What is accuracy? | How close a measurement is to the true value
How close a measurement is to the true value — what term is this? | Accuracy
How does precision differ from accuracy? | Precision: how close repeated measurements are to each other. Accuracy: how close a measurement is to the true value. Measurements can be precise but not accurate if each reading has the same systematic error
What does it mean for a measurement to be repeatable? | The original experimenter repeats the investigation using the same method and equipment and obtains the same results
The original experimenter repeats the investigation using the same method and equipment and obtains the same results — what term is this? | Repeatable
What does it mean for a measurement to be reproducible? | The investigation is repeated by another person, or using different equipment or techniques, and the same results are obtained
The investigation is repeated by another person, or using different equipment or techniques, and the same results are obtained — what term is this? | Reproducible
How does repeatability differ from reproducibility? | Repeatability: same experimenter, same method and equipment. Reproducibility: different person, or different equipment or techniques. Both require the same results
What is the resolution of a measuring instrument? | The smallest change in the quantity being measured that gives a perceptible change in the reading
The smallest change in the quantity being measured that gives a perceptible change in the reading — what term is this? | Resolution
What is the resolution of a typical wristwatch, and of a digital stopclock? | Wristwatch: 1 s. Digital stopclock: typically 10 ms (0.01 s)
How do uncertainties differ from errors? | Errors are issues with equipment or methodology that cause readings to differ from the true value. Uncertainty is a range of values within which the true value is expected to lie, and is an estimate
What is absolute uncertainty? | Uncertainty given as a fixed quantity in the same units as the measurement, denoted by $\Delta$
What is fractional uncertainty? | $\text{Fractional uncertainty} = \frac{\text{uncertainty}}{\text{measured value}}$
What is the equation for percentage uncertainty? | $\text{Percentage uncertainty} = \frac{\text{uncertainty}}{\text{measured value}} \times 100\%$
What is the uncertainty in a single reading from an analogue scale? | $\pm$ half the smallest division
What is the uncertainty in a measurement (e.g. using a ruler where two readings are taken)? | At least $\pm 1$ smallest division
What is the uncertainty in repeated data? | Half the range: $\pm \frac{1}{2}(\text{largest} - \text{smallest value})$
What is the uncertainty in a digital reading? | $\pm$ the last significant digit, unless otherwise quoted
How are uncertainties combined when adding or subtracting data? | Add the absolute uncertainties
How are uncertainties combined when multiplying or dividing data? | Add the percentage or fractional uncertainties
How is uncertainty handled when raising a quantity to a power? | Multiply the percentage uncertainty by the power
To how many significant figures should absolute uncertainties be quoted? | One significant figure
To how many significant figures should percentage and fractional uncertainties be quoted? | No more than two significant figures
How should the number of significant figures in a final result relate to the uncertainty? | The final result must be rounded to match the precision of the absolute uncertainty
What is the uncertainty in numbers and constants such as $\pi$? | Zero
What do error bars on a graph represent? | The absolute uncertainty in the plotted values
What is the 'best' line of best fit on a graph? | The line that passes as close as possible to all the data points
What is the 'worst' line of best fit on a graph? | The steepest or shallowest line that still fits within all the error bars
What is the equation for percentage uncertainty in a gradient from a graph? | $\frac{\text{best gradient} - \text{worst gradient}}{\text{best gradient}} \times 100\%$
What is the equation for percentage uncertainty in a y-intercept from a graph? | $\frac{\text{best y-intercept} - \text{worst y-intercept}}{\text{best y-intercept}} \times 100\%$
What are anomalies in experimental data, and how are they handled? | Readings that do not fit the expected pattern, identified through repeated measurements and omitted from the final results
