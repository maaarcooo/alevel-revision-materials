What is a cathode? | A negatively charged electrode
What is an anode? | A positively charged electrode
What is a discharge tube? | A glass tube of low-pressure gas with an anode at one end and a cathode at the other, connected to a high-voltage supply
What are cathode rays? | Beams of electrons emitted from the cathode
What showed that cathode rays are negatively charged particles? | Their deflection by a magnetic field in the direction expected for a negative charge
In Crookes' tube, a paddle wheel turned and moved towards the anode. What did this show about cathode rays? | They are particles carrying momentum and energy from the cathode towards the anode
How are cathode rays produced in a discharge tube? | The strong electric field ionises gas atoms. Positive ions accelerate into the cathode and release electrons, which are accelerated towards the anode
In a discharge tube, which way do the electrons and positive ions move? | Electrons move towards the anode, positive ions towards the cathode
Why must the gas in a discharge tube be at low pressure? | The particles are widely spaced, so charged particles can accelerate to high kinetic energy between collisions
Explain how recombination produces light in a discharge tube | Positive ions and electrons collide and recombine into atoms in excited states. These de-excite to the ground state, emitting photons
Explain how collisions between fast electrons and gas atoms produce light in a discharge tube | The electrons excite the gas atoms. The atoms then de-excite, emitting photons
What is thermionic emission? | The emission of electrons from a heated metal surface
The emission of electrons from a heated metal surface - what term is this? | Thermionic emission
Explain why heating a metal filament causes it to emit electrons | Its electrons gain enough kinetic energy to escape the metal surface
In thermionic emission, what is the role of the current through the filament? | It heats the filament. The high temperature, not the current itself, gives electrons enough energy to escape
What is an electron gun? | A device that emits electrons from a heated cathode and accelerates them towards an anode to form a beam
How does an electron gun produce a narrow beam of electrons? | Electrons accelerated towards the anode pass through a small hole in its centre
What pds does an electron gun use? | A low pd (a few volts) to heat the filament, and a high pd (hundreds of volts or more) to accelerate the electrons
What must surround the filament and anode of an electron gun, and why? | A vacuum or very low-pressure gas, so the electrons are not stopped or scattered by gas molecules
What speed do electrons reach in an electron gun with anode pd $V$? | $v = \sqrt{\frac{2eV}{m_e}}$, from $\frac{1}{2}m_e v^2 = eV$
What speed do electrons reach in an electron gun with an anode pd of 500 V? | $v = \sqrt{\frac{2 \times 1.60 \times 10^{-19} \times 500}{9.11 \times 10^{-31}}} = 1.33 \times 10^{7}\;\text{m s}^{-1}$
Why does an electron beam moving perpendicular to a uniform magnetic field follow a circular path? | The magnetic force is always perpendicular to the velocity, so it provides the centripetal force
In the magnetic-field method, what expression for electron speed comes from equating magnetic and centripetal force? | $v = \frac{Ber}{m_e}$, from $Bev = \frac{m_e v^2}{r}$
What is the specific charge of electrons accelerated through anode pd $V_A$ that then move in a circle of radius $r$ in a field $B$? | $\frac{e}{m_e} = \frac{2V_A}{B^2 r^2}$
Electrons accelerated through 200 V move in a circle of radius $4.8 \times 10^{-2}$ m in a $1.0 \times 10^{-3}$ T field. What is their specific charge? | $\frac{e}{m_e} = \frac{2V_A}{B^2 r^2} = \frac{2 \times 200}{(1.0 \times 10^{-3})^2 \times (4.8 \times 10^{-2})^2} = 1.7 \times 10^{11}\;\text{C kg}^{-1}$
Why does a fine beam tube contain a low-pressure gas? | Electrons excite the gas atoms, which emit light, making the circular path visible so its radius can be measured
In Thomson's crossed-field method, what produces the uniform magnetic field? | Helmholtz coils
In the crossed-field method, what condition makes the electron beam travel in a straight line? | The electric force is equal in magnitude and opposite in direction to the magnetic force, so the resultant force is zero
What is the speed of electrons passing undeflected through crossed fields with plate pd $V$, plate separation $d$ and flux density $B$? | $v = \frac{V}{Bd}$
Electrons pass undeflected between plates 5.0 cm apart with pd 200 V in a $2.0 \times 10^{-3}$ T magnetic field. What is their speed? | $v = \frac{V}{Bd} = \frac{200}{2.0 \times 10^{-3} \times 0.050} = 2.0 \times 10^{6}\;\text{m s}^{-1}$
In the crossed-field method, how is the radius $r$ of the beam's path found? | Switch off the electric field and measure the radius of the circular path in the magnetic field alone
What expression for specific charge does the crossed-field method give? | $\frac{e}{m_e} = \frac{V}{B^2 r d}$
In the crossed-field method, how is the specific charge found from the accelerating pd $V_A$? | $\frac{e}{m_e} = \frac{v^2}{2V_A}$, with $v = \frac{V}{Bd}$ from the balanced fields
For an electron beam deflected between parallel plates, which component of its velocity stays constant? | The component perpendicular to the electric field
How long does an electron with speed $v$ parallel to the plates spend between plates of length $w$? | $t = \frac{w}{v}$
What is the acceleration of an electron between parallel plates separated by $d$ with pd $V$? | $a = \frac{eV}{d m_e}$
What is the vertical deflection $y$ of an electron leaving plates of length $w$, having entered parallel to them at speed $v$? | $y = \frac{1}{2}a\frac{w^2}{v^2}$
How is the specific charge found from the electrons' vertical acceleration $a$ between the plates? | $\frac{e}{m_e} = \frac{ad}{V}$, from $m_e a = \frac{eV}{d}$
How did Thomson's specific charge for cathode-ray particles compare with that of the hydrogen ion, the largest then known? | It was much larger, about 1800 times
What two explanations could account for the electron's specific charge being much larger than the hydrogen ion's? | The electron has a much smaller mass, or a much larger charge
Why could a measurement of $\frac{e}{m_e}$ alone not show whether the electron had a small mass or a large charge? | Specific charge is a ratio, so charge and mass cannot be separated from it
What did Millikan's oil drop experiment determine? | The charge of the electron
Why did Millikan use oil drops rather than water drops? | Oil does not evaporate quickly, so each drop's mass stays constant
How are the oil drops observed in Millikan's experiment? | Through a microscope
What is the condition for a charged oil drop to be held stationary between parallel plates? | $\frac{QV}{d} = mg$
What is adjusted to hold a charged oil drop stationary between the plates? | The potential difference between the plates
An oil drop of mass $1.6 \times 10^{-15}$ kg is held stationary between plates 10 mm apart with a pd of 980 V. What is its charge? | $Q = \frac{mgd}{V} = \frac{1.6 \times 10^{-15} \times 9.81 \times 0.010}{980} = 1.6 \times 10^{-19}\;\text{C}$
What is Stokes' law? | The viscous drag on a sphere of radius $r$ moving at speed $v$ through a fluid of viscosity $\eta$ is $F = 6\pi\eta r v$
Under what conditions does Stokes' law apply? | A small, spherical object moving at low speed
What is the unit of viscosity? | Pa s (N s m$^{-2}$)
What is the drag on a sphere of radius $1.0 \times 10^{-6}$ m moving at $1.0 \times 10^{-4}$ m s$^{-1}$ through air of viscosity $1.8 \times 10^{-5}$ Pa s? | $F = 6\pi\eta r v = 6\pi \times 1.8 \times 10^{-5} \times 1.0 \times 10^{-6} \times 1.0 \times 10^{-4} = 3.4 \times 10^{-14}\;\text{N}$
Why are the oil drops allowed to fall with the electric field switched off? | To measure their terminal velocity, from which each drop's radius and mass are found
How is the terminal velocity of an oil drop measured? | By timing the drop as it falls a known distance
What forces balance on an oil drop falling at terminal velocity with no electric field? | Weight and viscous drag: $mg = 6\pi\eta r v$
What forces balance on an oil drop rising at constant speed $v$ with the field on? | $\frac{QV}{d} = mg + 6\pi\eta r v$
What is the mass of a spherical oil drop of radius $r$ and density $\rho$? | $m = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3 \rho$
What expression gives the radius of an oil drop falling at terminal velocity $v$ with no electric field? | $r = \sqrt{\frac{9\eta v}{2\rho g}}$
What two assumptions are made in finding an oil drop's radius from its terminal velocity? | The drop is spherical, and the upthrust from the air is negligible
Which two measurements on each oil drop let Millikan find its charge? | Its terminal velocity with the field off, and the pd needed to hold it stationary
What pattern did Millikan find in the charges on the oil drops? | Every charge was an integer multiple of $1.60 \times 10^{-19}$ C
What is meant by quantisation of electric charge? | Charge exists only in integer multiples of the electron charge $e$
Why does a charged oil drop always carry an integer multiple of $e$? | It has gained or lost a whole number of electrons
