What are hadrons? | Subatomic particles made up of quarks that can feel the strong nuclear force
Subatomic particles made up of quarks that can feel the strong nuclear force — what term is this? | Hadrons
What are the two classes of hadrons? | Baryons (three quarks) and mesons (a quark and anti-quark pair)
What are the two most common baryons? | Protons and neutrons
What are the two most common mesons? | Pions and kaons
What is an anti-baryon made of? | Three anti-quarks
How does the anti-particle of a meson relate to the original meson? | It is still a quark and anti-quark pair, but the quark becomes the anti-quark and vice versa
In what combinations are quarks always observed? | In pairs (mesons) or groups of three (baryons), never in isolation
Why must the quarks in a baryon be either all quarks or all anti-quarks? | A mixture would produce a non-integer charge and a non-integer baryon number
What is the baryon number of a baryon? | $B = +1$
What is the baryon number of an anti-baryon? | $B = -1$
What is the baryon number of a particle that is not a baryon (e.g. mesons, leptons)? | $B = 0$
In which interactions is baryon number conserved? | All interactions
What is the baryon number of an individual quark? | $B = +\frac{1}{3}$
What is the baryon number of an individual anti-quark? | $B = -\frac{1}{3}$
Why is the proton the most stable baryon? | It is the lightest baryon, so it cannot decay into a lighter baryon
What do all other baryons eventually decay into? | Protons
Why would proton decay violate a conservation law? | No lighter baryon exists for it to decay into, so baryon number could not be conserved
What is the theorised half-life of the proton? | Around $10^{32}$ years
What three charge states can pions have? | Positive ($\pi^+$), negative ($\pi^-$), and neutral ($\pi^0$)
What types of quarks do pions contain? | Up and down quarks and their anti-quarks only
What is the strangeness of all pions? | $S = 0$ (they contain no strange quarks)
What is the quark composition of $\pi^+$? | $u\bar{d}$
What is the quark composition of $\pi^-$? | $d\bar{u}$
What is the quark composition of $\pi^0$? | $u\bar{u}$ or $d\bar{d}$
What is the antiparticle of $\pi^+$? | $\pi^-$
What is the antiparticle of $\pi^0$? | Itself (the neutral pion is its own antiparticle)
Why are pions more stable than other types of mesons? | They are the lightest mesons
What is the exchange particle of the strong nuclear force between baryons? | The pion
How do gluons and pions differ as mediators of the strong force? | Gluons bind quarks together (strong interaction); pions bind nucleons together (strong nuclear force)
What three charge states can kaons have? | Positive ($K^+$), negative ($K^-$), and neutral ($K^0$)
What is the quark composition of $K^+$? | $u\bar{s}$
What is the quark composition of $K^-$? | $s\bar{u}$
What is the quark composition of $K^0$? | $d\bar{s}$
What is the quark composition of $\bar{K}^0$? | $s\bar{d}$
What is the antiparticle of $K^+$? | $K^-$
How does the neutral kaon differ from the neutral pion regarding antiparticles? | The neutral kaon is not its own antiparticle ($\bar{K}^0$ is distinct from $K^0$), whereas the neutral pion is its own antiparticle
What do kaons typically decay into? | Pions
Give an example equation for kaon pair production | $p + p \rightarrow p + p + K^+ + K^-$
Give an example equation for kaon decay | $K^0 \rightarrow \pi^+ + \pi^-$
What are leptons? | Fundamental (elementary) particles that are not made up of quarks
Fundamental particles that are not made up of quarks and do not interact via the strong force — what term is this? | Leptons
Through which forces do leptons interact? | Weak, gravitational, and electromagnetic (not the strong nuclear force)
Name the two charged leptons at A-level | Electron ($e^-$) and muon ($\mu^-$)
What are the two neutrinos at A-level? | Electron neutrino ($\nu_e$) and muon neutrino ($\nu_\mu$)
How does the muon compare to the electron? | Same charge ($-1e$) and similar properties, but the muon is heavier (about $0.1\,u$ vs $0.0005\,u$)
What is the charge and approximate mass of a neutrino? | Zero charge and negligible mass (almost zero)
What interaction distinguishes quarks from leptons? | The strong force (quarks feel it, leptons do not)
What are the most abundant leptons in the universe? | Neutrinos
What is the lepton number of a lepton? | $L = +1$
What is the lepton number of an anti-lepton? | $L = -1$
What is the lepton number of a non-lepton? | $L = 0$
In which interactions is lepton number conserved? | All interactions
Write the equation for muon decay | $\mu^- \rightarrow e^- + \bar{\nu}_e + \nu_\mu$
What does an anti-muon ($\mu^+$) typically decay into? | A positron
Through which interaction does muon decay occur? | The weak interaction (mediated by the $W^-$ boson)
What are the three flavours of quark at A-level? | Up ($u$), down ($d$), and strange ($s$)
What is the charge of an up quark? | $+\frac{2}{3}\,e$
What is the charge of a down quark? | $-\frac{1}{3}\,e$
What is the charge of a strange quark? | $-\frac{1}{3}\,e$
How do the properties of anti-quarks compare to their corresponding quarks? | Identical but with opposite charge, baryon number, and strangeness
How is the charge of a hadron determined from its quarks? | By summing the charges of its constituent quarks
What is the quark composition of a proton? | $uud$
What is the quark composition of a neutron? | $udd$
What is strangeness? | A quantum number: $S = -1$ for a strange quark, $S = +1$ for an anti-strange quark, $S = 0$ if neither is present
A quantum number assigned based on whether a particle contains a strange or anti-strange quark — what is this? | Strangeness ($S$)
In which interactions is strangeness conserved? | All interactions except the weak interaction
By how much can strangeness change in a weak interaction? | By $0$, $+1$, or $-1$
What defines a strange particle? | It contains a strange or anti-strange quark (e.g. kaons)
Through which interaction are strange particles always produced? | The strong interaction
Through which interaction do strange particles always decay? | The weak interaction
Why must strange particles be produced in pairs? | Strangeness is conserved in the strong interaction, so a strange quark must be produced alongside an anti-strange quark
Why was the neutrino first hypothesised? | To account for energy conservation in beta decay
How is a theory validated in particle physics? | The discovery is confirmed by repeat experiments that reduce experimental uncertainty, leading the scientific community to accept it
What are particle accelerators used for? | Colliding particles at very high speeds to produce new particles and reveal the inner structure of particles
What are the two types of particle accelerator? | Linear and circular
What is the Large Hadron Collider (LHC)? | The world's largest and highest-energy particle collider, based at CERN on the France-Switzerland border
Why does particle physics research require international collaboration? | The equipment is extremely expensive and complex, requiring large groups of scientists and engineers to provide funding and expertise
