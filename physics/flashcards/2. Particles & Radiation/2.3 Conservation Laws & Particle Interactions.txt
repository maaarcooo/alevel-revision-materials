What are the four fundamental interactions? | Gravity, electromagnetism, strong nuclear (strong interaction), and weak nuclear
Which is the strongest fundamental interaction? | The strong nuclear interaction
Which is the weakest fundamental interaction? | Gravity
What is the range of the electromagnetic and gravitational interactions? | Infinite
What is the range of the weak force? | Up to $10^{-18}$ m
What is the range of the strong force? | Approximately $10^{-15}$ m
What property must particles have to be affected by the gravitational interaction? | Mass
What property must particles have to be affected by the electromagnetic interaction? | Charge
Which particles are affected by the weak interaction? | All hadrons and all leptons
Which particles are affected by the strong interaction? | Hadrons only (particles made of quarks)
Why does gravity not play a part in particle interactions? | It is so weak that it only has a noticeable effect on large masses, not individual particles
What are exchange particles? | Virtual particles that exist briefly to carry (mediate) the fundamental force between interacting particles
Virtual particles that exist briefly to mediate the fundamental force between interacting particles — what term is this? | Exchange particles (also called gauge bosons)
What is the exchange particle of the strong interaction between nucleons? | Pion ($\pi^+$, $\pi^-$, $\pi^0$)
What is the exchange particle of the strong interaction between quarks? | Gluon
How do pions and gluons differ as exchange particles? | Pions carry the strong nuclear force between nucleons, while gluons carry the strong interaction between quarks
What are the exchange particles of the weak interaction? | $W^+$ boson, $W^-$ boson, or $Z^0$ boson
Why are beta decays caused by the weak interaction? | A quark changes type, which only the weak interaction can do
What is the exchange particle of the electromagnetic interaction? | Virtual photon ($\gamma$)
What is exchanged when two electrons repel each other? | A virtual photon
How does the exchange of a virtual photon make two electrons repel? | The virtual photon carries momentum from one electron to the other, so by conservation of momentum the path of each electron changes
What is the theorised exchange particle of the gravitational force? | The graviton (not yet discovered)
State three properties of the photon | No mass, no charge, and it is its own antiparticle
Why can leptons not interact via the strong force? | Leptons are not made of quarks, and the strong force only acts on particles made of quarks (hadrons)
Explain why the electromagnetic force is responsible for binding electrons to atoms | The attractive force between the negatively charged electrons and the positively charged nucleus holds the electrons in place
What is the weak interaction responsible for? | Beta decay, electron capture and electron-proton collisions, in which a quark changes type
Write the equation for $\beta^-$ decay | $n \rightarrow p + e^- + \bar{\nu}_e$
What is the exchange particle in $\beta^-$ decay? | $W^-$ boson
Write the equation for $\beta^+$ decay | $p \rightarrow n + e^+ + \nu_e$
What is the exchange particle in $\beta^+$ decay? | $W^+$ boson
How does the sign of the W boson relate to the type of beta decay? | The sign matches: $W^-$ is exchanged in $\beta^-$ decay, $W^+$ is exchanged in $\beta^+$ decay
What is electron capture? | A process where an atomic electron is absorbed by a proton in the nucleus, producing a neutron and an electron neutrino
A process where an atomic electron is absorbed by a proton in the nucleus, producing a neutron and an electron neutrino — what term is this? | Electron capture
Write the equation for electron capture | $p + e^- \rightarrow n + \nu_e$
What is the exchange particle in electron capture? | $W^+$ boson (the proton acts on the electron)
What is the exchange particle in an electron-proton collision? | $W^-$ boson (the electron acts on the proton)
How does electron capture differ from an electron-proton collision? | Same equation ($p + e^- \rightarrow n + \nu_e$), but in electron capture the proton acts on the electron (mediated by $W^+$), while in a collision the electron acts on the proton (mediated by $W^-$)
Which fundamental interaction facilitates the collision in electron-proton interactions? | The weak interaction (although the particles are attracted via the electromagnetic interaction)
What do the y-axis and x-axis represent on a Feynman diagram? | y-axis represents time, x-axis represents space
What is a vertex on a Feynman diagram? | The point where particles and exchange particles meet, representing a point of interaction
How are particles represented on a Feynman diagram? | Straight lines with arrows pointing forward in time
How are exchange particles represented on a Feynman diagram? | Wavy lines with no arrows (charged W bosons may have a directional arrow above the line)
Where do incoming and outgoing particles appear on a Feynman diagram? | Incoming particles enter at the bottom, outgoing particles leave at the top
In what default direction is the exchange particle transferred on a Feynman diagram? | Left to right, unless an arrow above the wavy line indicates otherwise
On a Feynman diagram, where are hadrons/quarks and leptons placed? | Hadrons/quarks on the left, leptons on the right; they must never meet at a vertex
What quantities must be conserved at each vertex of a Feynman diagram? | Charge, baryon number, and lepton number
State the rule about line crossing on Feynman diagrams | Lines must not cross over
What quark change occurs in $\beta^-$ decay? | A down quark changes to an up quark, converting a neutron (udd) to a proton (uud)
What quark change occurs in $\beta^+$ decay? | An up quark changes to a down quark, converting a proton (uud) to a neutron (udd)
What role does the $W^-$ boson play in $\beta^-$ decay at the quark level? | It carries negative charge away from the down quark as it becomes an up quark, then decays into the electron and the electron antineutrino
What role does the $W^+$ boson play in $\beta^+$ decay at the quark level? | It carries positive charge away from the up quark as it becomes a down quark, then decays into the positron and the electron neutrino
Which quantities are always conserved in particle interactions? | Charge (Q), baryon number (B), lepton number (L), energy (mass-energy), and momentum
Which quantity does not need to be conserved in weak interactions? | Strangeness (S); it can change by 0, +1, or $-1$
How do you determine whether a particle interaction is permitted? | Check that each quantum number (Q, B, L, S) is conserved on both sides of the equation; if any is not conserved (except S in weak interactions), the interaction cannot occur
