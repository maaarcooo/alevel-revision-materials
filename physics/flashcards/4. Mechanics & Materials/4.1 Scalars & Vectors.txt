What is a scalar quantity? | A quantity that has magnitude but not direction
A quantity that has magnitude but not direction — what term is this? | Scalar quantity
What is a vector quantity? | A quantity that has both magnitude and direction
A quantity that has both magnitude and direction — what term is this? | Vector quantity
How can an object have constant speed but changing velocity? | If the object is changing direction, its velocity changes even though its speed remains the same
Classify distance as scalar or vector | Scalar
Classify displacement as scalar or vector | Vector
Classify speed as scalar or vector | Scalar
Classify velocity as scalar or vector | Vector
Classify mass as scalar or vector | Scalar
Classify weight as scalar or vector | Vector (it is a force with both magnitude and direction)
Classify acceleration as scalar or vector | Vector
Classify force as scalar or vector | Vector
Classify momentum as scalar or vector | Vector
Classify energy as scalar or vector | Scalar
Classify electric charge as scalar or vector | Scalar
Classify temperature as scalar or vector | Scalar
Classify pressure as scalar or vector | Scalar
How are vectors represented in diagrams? | By arrows: the arrowhead shows the direction and the length represents the magnitude
What is a resultant vector? | The single vector produced by adding two or more vectors together. Also called the net vector
The single vector produced by adding two or more vectors — what is it called? | The resultant vector (or net vector)
When two perpendicular vectors are added, how is the magnitude of the resultant found? | Using Pythagoras' theorem: $R = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$
When two perpendicular vectors are added, how is the direction of the resultant found? | Using trigonometry, e.g. $\tan\theta = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}$
A hiker walks 6 km east and then 10 km north. What is the magnitude and direction of the displacement? | $\sqrt{6^2 + 10^2} = 11.7$ km, at $\tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{10}{6}\right) = 59°$ north of east
When two vectors are not at right angles, how is the resultant vector found? | By scale drawing: draw the vectors to scale, link them head-to-tail, measure the resultant's length with a ruler and its angle with a protractor
In the triangle method of vector addition, how are the vectors arranged? | Head-to-tail. The resultant is drawn from the tail of the first vector to the head of the last
In the parallelogram method of vector addition, how are the vectors arranged? | Tail-to-tail. The parallelogram is completed and the resultant is the diagonal from the shared starting point
How is vector subtraction ($\mathbf{a} - \mathbf{b}$) performed? | Reverse the direction of $\mathbf{b}$ to get $-\mathbf{b}$, then add $\mathbf{a}$ and $-\mathbf{b}$ using the triangle or parallelogram method
What is meant by resolving a vector? | Splitting a single vector into two perpendicular components that together have the same effect as the original
What are the two perpendicular parts of a resolved vector called? | Components
What is the horizontal component of a force $F$ acting at angle $\theta$ to the horizontal? | $F_x = F\cos\theta$
What is the vertical component of a force $F$ acting at angle $\theta$ to the horizontal? | $F_y = F\sin\theta$
A force of 50 N acts at 30° to the horizontal. What are its horizontal and vertical components? | $50\cos 30° = 43$ N horizontally and $50\sin 30° = 25$ N vertically
Why is it useful to resolve a vector into perpendicular components? | Perpendicular components do not affect each other, so each direction can be treated separately
On an inclined plane at angle $\theta$, what is the component of weight parallel to the slope? | $W\sin\theta$
On an inclined plane at angle $\theta$, what is the component of weight perpendicular to the slope? | $W\cos\theta$
On a frictionless inclined plane at angle $\theta$, what causes the object to accelerate down the slope? | The component of weight parallel to the slope, $W\sin\theta$
On an inclined plane at angle $\theta$, what does the normal reaction force equal when there is no acceleration perpendicular to the slope? | $R = W\cos\theta$
When are the forces on an object in equilibrium? | When the resultant force on it is zero, so it is at rest or moving at constant velocity
How are coplanar forces in equilibrium represented in a vector diagram? | As a closed vector triangle: the vectors joined head-to-tail form a closed path
What does coplanar mean? | Acting in the same plane
What are the two ways of showing that an object is in equilibrium? | Show that the horizontal and vertical components each add to zero, or draw the forces to scale and show that they form a closed triangle
