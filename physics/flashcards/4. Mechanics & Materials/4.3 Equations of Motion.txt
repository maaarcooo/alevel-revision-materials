What is distance? | The total length between two points (scalar quantity)
What is speed? | The total distance travelled per unit of time (scalar quantity)
What is displacement? | The distance of an object from a fixed point in a specified direction (vector quantity)
The distance of an object from a fixed point in a specified direction — what quantity is this? | Displacement
What is velocity? | The rate of change of displacement of an object
The rate of change of displacement of an object — what quantity is this? | Velocity
What is acceleration? | The rate of change of velocity of an object
The rate of change of velocity of an object — what quantity is this? | Acceleration
How do scalar and vector quantities differ? | Scalar quantities have magnitude only; vector quantities have both magnitude and direction
What is the equation linking velocity, displacement, and time? | $v = \frac{\Delta s}{\Delta t}$
What is the equation linking acceleration, velocity, and time? | $a = \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}$
What is the SI unit of velocity? | $\text{m s}^{-1}$
What is the SI unit of acceleration? | $\text{m s}^{-2}$
In physics, what does the symbol $\Delta$ mean? | Change in (a quantity)
What is instantaneous velocity? | The velocity of an object at a specific point in time
How is instantaneous velocity found on a displacement-time graph? | Draw a tangent at the required time and calculate its gradient
What is average speed? | Total distance divided by total time
How is average velocity found on a displacement-time graph? | Divide the total displacement (y-axis) by the total time (x-axis)
What does the gradient of a displacement-time graph represent? | Velocity
What does the y-intercept of a displacement-time graph represent? | Initial displacement
On a displacement-time graph, what does a straight diagonal line represent? | Constant velocity
On a displacement-time graph, what does a curved line represent? | Acceleration (changing velocity)
On a displacement-time graph, what does a horizontal line represent? | The object is at rest
What does the area under a displacement-time graph represent? | Nothing — it has no physical meaning
On a displacement-time graph, what does the sign of the gradient indicate? | The direction of motion: positive gradient means motion in the positive direction, negative gradient means motion in the negative direction
How does a displacement-time graph differ from a distance-time graph? | A displacement-time graph can have positive or negative values on the y-axis; a distance-time graph has only positive values
What does the gradient of a velocity-time graph represent? | Acceleration
What does the y-intercept of a velocity-time graph represent? | Initial velocity
On a velocity-time graph, what does a straight line represent? | Uniform (constant) acceleration
On a velocity-time graph, what does a curved line represent? | Non-uniform acceleration
On a velocity-time graph, what does a horizontal line represent? | Constant velocity (zero acceleration)
What does the area under a velocity-time graph represent? | Displacement or distance travelled
How does a velocity-time graph differ from a speed-time graph? | A velocity-time graph can show positive or negative values on the y-axis; a speed-time graph has only positive values
What does the gradient of an acceleration-time graph represent? | It has no physical meaning
What does the y-intercept of an acceleration-time graph represent? | Initial acceleration
On an acceleration-time graph, what does a horizontal line represent? | Constant acceleration
What does the area under an acceleration-time graph represent? | Change in velocity
For a bouncing ball in a uniform gravitational field (no air resistance), in what direction does the acceleration act throughout the motion? | Always downwards, constant at $g$, regardless of whether the ball is rising or falling
What happens to a bouncing ball's velocity at the highest point of its trajectory? | The velocity is momentarily zero, then changes from positive (upward) to negative (downward) as the ball changes direction
At the point of a bounce, what happens to a ball's velocity and speed? | Velocity changes instantaneously from negative to positive (direction reverses), but speed (magnitude) remains the same
What does the acronym SUVAT stand for? | $s$ (displacement), $u$ (initial velocity), $v$ (final velocity), $a$ (acceleration), $t$ (time)
When can the SUVAT equations be used? | For motion with constant acceleration in a straight line
State the SUVAT equation that does not contain $s$ | $v = u + at$
State the SUVAT equation that does not contain $v$ | $s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2$
State the SUVAT equation that does not contain $a$ | $s = \frac{(v + u)}{2}t$
State the SUVAT equation that does not contain $t$ | $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$
In SUVAT problems, what does "starts from rest" imply? | $u = 0$ (initial velocity is zero)
In SUVAT problems, if an object is only falling under gravity, what value is used for $a$? | $a = g = 9.81 \text{ m s}^{-2}$
In the SUVAT equations, which is the only variable that is not a vector? | Time, $t$ — all other variables ($s$, $u$, $v$, $a$) are vectors and can be positive or negative
Why must the sign convention be consistent for all vector quantities in a SUVAT problem? | Because inconsistent signs give incorrect answers — if upward is positive then $s$ upward is positive and $a$ downward must be negative, or vice versa
What is a projectile? | A particle moving freely (non-powered) under gravity in a two-dimensional plane
In projectile motion, how are the horizontal and vertical components related? | They are independent of each other and must be evaluated separately using SUVAT equations
What is the horizontal acceleration of a projectile (ignoring air resistance)? | Zero — horizontal velocity remains constant throughout the flight
What is the vertical acceleration of a projectile (ignoring air resistance)? | $g = 9.81 \text{ m s}^{-2}$ directed downwards
For a projectile launched at angle $\theta$ with speed $u$, what is the initial vertical component of velocity? | $u_y = u\sin\theta$
For a projectile launched at angle $\theta$ with speed $u$, what is the initial horizontal component of velocity? | $u_x = u\cos\theta$
At what point in its trajectory does a projectile reach its maximum height? | When the vertical component of velocity equals zero, which occurs at half the total time of flight
What is the equation for the total time of flight of a projectile launched at angle $\theta$ with speed $u$? | $T = \frac{2u\sin\theta}{g}$
What is the equation for the maximum height of a projectile launched at angle $\theta$ with speed $u$? | $H = \frac{(u\sin\theta)^2}{2g}$
What is the equation for the range of a projectile launched at angle $\theta$ with speed $u$? | $R = \frac{u^2\sin 2\theta}{g}$
What is time of flight for a projectile? | The total time the projectile spends in the air
What is the range of a projectile? | The total horizontal distance travelled by the projectile
What is the maximum height attained by a projectile? | The highest vertical point reached, where the vertical velocity component is momentarily zero
How does air resistance affect a projectile's trajectory? | It reduces both the horizontal range and the maximum height, and causes a steeper descent path
What two factors most affect the motion of an object through a fluid? | Mass (determines weight) and surface area (determines drag force)
What is a fluid? | A term used to describe a liquid or a gas
A term used to describe a liquid or a gas — what is this? | A fluid
What are drag forces? | Forces that oppose the motion of an object moving through a fluid (gas or liquid)
In what direction do drag forces act? | In the opposite direction to the motion of the object
How does drag force change with the speed of an object? | Drag force increases as the speed increases
What energy conversions occur due to drag forces? | Kinetic energy is converted into heat and sound
What is lift? | An upward force on an object moving through a fluid, acting perpendicular to the fluid flow
Explain how lift is generated on an aeroplane wing | The wing pushes air downward as it moves; by Newton's third law, the air exerts an equal and opposite upward force (lift) on the wing
Why do aeroplanes fly at high altitudes? | The air is less dense at higher altitudes, so there is less air resistance
In an exam, what does it mean if air resistance is described as "negligible"? | There are no drag forces acting on the body — air resistance is too small to affect the motion
What is terminal velocity? | The maximum constant velocity of a falling object, reached when the drag force equals the weight and the resultant force and acceleration are zero
The maximum velocity reached by a falling object when drag equals weight — what is this called? | Terminal velocity
Explain why a falling object eventually reaches terminal velocity | As speed increases, drag force increases, reducing the resultant force and acceleration. When drag equals weight, the resultant force is zero and velocity becomes constant
On a velocity-time graph, how is the approach to terminal velocity shown? | The curve starts steep (large gradient), the gradient decreases as drag increases, and the line levels off to horizontal at terminal velocity
Why does a heavier skydiver have a higher terminal velocity than a lighter one? | Greater weight means drag must reach a larger value to equal the weight, which requires a higher speed
What is the aim of the required practical on determination of $g$? | To calculate the acceleration due to gravity by measuring the time for a ball-bearing to fall a known distance, then using an equation of motion
In the determination of $g$ experiment, what is the independent variable? | Height, $h$ (the distance between the two light gates)
In the determination of $g$ experiment, what is the dependent variable? | Time, $t$ (the time for the ball-bearing to fall between the two light gates)
Name three control variables in the determination of $g$ experiment | Same steel ball-bearing, same electromagnet, and same distance between the ball-bearing and the top of the glass tube
What is the purpose of the electromagnet in the determination of $g$ experiment? | To hold and release the ball-bearing consistently from rest at a fixed position above the glass tube
What is the purpose of the light gates in the determination of $g$ experiment? | To accurately measure the fall time — the first gate starts the timer and the second stops it
What is the purpose of the glass tube in the determination of $g$ experiment? | To guide the ball-bearing vertically downwards in a straight line
What is the purpose of the cushion in the determination of $g$ experiment? | To catch the ball-bearing and prevent it from being damaged or damaging the surface
What are the resolutions of the measuring instruments in the determination of $g$ experiment? | Metre ruler: 1 mm; timer (via light gates): 0.01 s
What graph is plotted to determine $g$ from the free-fall experiment? | $\frac{2h}{t}$ on the y-axis against $t$ on the x-axis
In the determination of $g$ graph, what does the gradient represent? | The acceleration due to gravity, $g$
In the determination of $g$ graph, what does the y-intercept represent? | $2u$, where $u$ is the initial velocity of the ball-bearing
Which SUVAT equation is rearranged for the determination of $g$ graph, and what form does it take? | $s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2$ rearranges to $\frac{2h}{t} = gt + 2u$, which has the form $y = mx + c$
Name one systematic error in the determination of $g$ experiment | Residual magnetism after the electromagnet is switched off delays the ball's release, making the recorded time longer than it should be
Name two random errors in the determination of $g$ experiment | Parallax error when reading height $h$ on the metre ruler, and the ball not falling accurately through the centre of each light gate
How are random errors reduced in the determination of $g$ experiment? | Repeat the experiment at least 3-5 times for each value of $h$ and calculate an average time
How is the uncertainty in $g$ determined from the graph? | The fractional uncertainty equals the difference between the gradients of the best fit and worst fit lines, divided by the gradient of the best fit line
What is the line of worst fit? | The steepest or shallowest straight line that still passes through all the error bars on the graph
How is the uncertainty in $h$ determined in the determination of $g$ experiment? | Half the resolution of the metre ruler (typically 0.5 mm)
How is the uncertainty in $t$ determined for each height in the determination of $g$ experiment? | Half the range of the repeated time measurements
How is the percentage uncertainty in $\frac{2h}{t}$ calculated? | Add the percentage uncertainties of $h$ and $t$ together
State two safety precautions in the determination of $g$ experiment | Keep water away from the electromagnet to avoid electrocution risk, and place a cushion below the glass tube to catch the ball-bearing
Why should the clamp stand be secured with a G clamp in the determination of $g$ experiment? | To keep the apparatus rigid so the glass tube stays vertical and the ball-bearing falls straight
Why should the current to the electromagnet only be switched on once all apparatus is set up? | To reduce the risk of electrocution
