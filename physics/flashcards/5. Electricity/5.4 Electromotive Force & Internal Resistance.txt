What is electromotive force (EMF)? | The amount of chemical energy converted to electrical energy per coulomb of charge when passing through a power supply, $\varepsilon = \frac{E}{Q}$
The amount of chemical energy converted to electrical energy per coulomb of charge when passing through a power supply - what quantity is this? | Electromotive force (EMF)
A cell transfers 9.0 J of energy to 6.0 C of charge. What is its EMF? | $\varepsilon = \frac{E}{Q} = \frac{9.0}{6.0} = 1.5$ V
What symbol represents EMF? | $\varepsilon$ (Greek letter epsilon)
In what unit is EMF measured? | Volts (V)
How is EMF measured? | Connect a high-resistance voltmeter in parallel with the cell in an open circuit (no current flowing)
What is terminal potential difference? | The potential difference across the terminals of a cell, given by $V = IR$ where $R$ is the external resistance
The potential difference across the terminals of a cell - what term is this? | Terminal potential difference (terminal PD)
When is the terminal PD of a cell equal to its EMF? | When no current flows, because there are then no lost volts
What is internal resistance? | The resistance of the materials within the battery
The resistance of the materials within the battery - what term is this? | Internal resistance
Explain why a cell becomes warm during use | Internal resistance causes the circulating charge to dissipate electrical energy within the power supply itself
What are lost volts? | The work done per unit charge to overcome the internal resistance inside the battery when current flows, $v = Ir$
The work done per unit charge to overcome the internal resistance inside the battery - what quantity is this? | Lost volts
How are EMF, terminal PD and lost volts related? | $\varepsilon = V + v$, so the terminal PD is $V = \varepsilon - Ir$
Why does the terminal PD of a cell fall as the current drawn from it increases? | The lost volts, $Ir$, increase, leaving less of the EMF across the external circuit
How is the power supplied by a cell shared in a circuit with internal resistance? | $\varepsilon I = I^2R + I^2r$: the power supplied by the cell equals the power delivered to the load plus the power wasted in the internal resistance
State the EMF equation for a circuit with internal resistance | $\varepsilon = IR + Ir = I(R + r)$, where $R$ = load resistance, $r$ = internal resistance
How is a cell with internal resistance modelled in a circuit? | As a source of EMF in series with an internal resistance $r$
What does "negligible internal resistance" imply in an exam question? | The EMF equals the terminal PD, so no internal resistance calculations are needed
A battery has EMF 7.3 V and internal resistance 0.3 $\Omega$, connected to a 9.5 $\Omega$ resistor. Calculate the current | $I = \frac{\varepsilon}{R + r} = \frac{7.3}{9.5 + 0.3} = 0.74$ A (2 s.f.)
A cell of EMF 1.5 V has a terminal PD of 1.2 V when it delivers 0.50 A. What is its internal resistance? | $r = \frac{\varepsilon - V}{I} = \frac{1.5 - 1.2}{0.50} = 0.60\;\Omega$
What is the aim of the EMF and internal resistance required practical? | To investigate the relationship between EMF and internal resistance by measuring the variation of current and voltage using a variable resistor
In the EMF and internal resistance experiment, what is the independent variable? | Resistance of the variable resistor, $R$ ($\Omega$)
In the EMF and internal resistance experiment, what are the dependent variables? | Voltage $V$ (V) and current $I$ (A)
In the EMF and internal resistance experiment, what are the control variables? | EMF of the cell and internal resistance of the cell
How are the ammeter and voltmeter connected in the EMF experiment? | Ammeter in series with the circuit, voltmeter in parallel across the variable resistor
Describe the procedure for collecting data in the EMF experiment | Set the variable resistor to maximum, close the switch, and record $V$ and $I$. Vary the resistance for 8-10 readings across the full range, opening the switch between each reading
What graph is plotted in the EMF experiment, and what do the gradient and y-intercept represent? | $V$ against $I$. Gradient $= -r$ (negative internal resistance), y-intercept $= \varepsilon$ (EMF)
Which equation gives the straight-line graph in the EMF experiment? | $V = -rI + \varepsilon$, which has the form $y = mx + c$
Why is the switch opened between readings in the EMF experiment? | To prevent the cell from heating up, which would change the internal resistance and introduce systematic error
Why might a fixed resistor be put in series with the variable resistor in the EMF experiment? | It limits the current when the variable resistor is set to zero, so the cell does not overheat or run down quickly
Why should fairly new cells be used in the EMF experiment? | Run-down batteries have EMF and internal resistance that vary during the experiment, introducing random error
Why are readings allowed to stabilise before recording in the EMF experiment? | To reduce random error from fluctuating voltmeter and ammeter readings
How are random errors minimised in the EMF experiment? | Take at least 3 repeat readings for each resistance value and calculate a mean
State the safety precautions for the EMF experiment | Components can get hot with prolonged use, so switch off the power supply if burning is smelt. Keep liquids away from the electrical equipment
