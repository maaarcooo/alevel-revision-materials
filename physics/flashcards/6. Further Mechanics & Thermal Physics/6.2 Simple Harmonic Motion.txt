What is simple harmonic motion? | An oscillation in which the acceleration is directly proportional to the displacement from the equilibrium position and is always directed towards it
An oscillation in which the acceleration is directly proportional to the displacement from the equilibrium position and is always directed towards it - what term is this? | Simple harmonic motion (SHM)
State the condition for simple harmonic motion as a proportionality | $a \propto -x$
What is the defining equation of simple harmonic motion? | $a = -\omega^2 x$
What does the minus sign in $a = -\omega^2 x$ show? | The acceleration is always in the opposite direction to the displacement, towards the equilibrium position
What is $\omega$ called in the equations of simple harmonic motion? | The angular frequency, measured in $\text{rad s}^{-1}$
What is the equilibrium position of an oscillator? | The position at which the resultant force on it is zero
What is the amplitude of an oscillation? | The maximum displacement from the equilibrium position
What is the restoring force on an oscillator? | The resultant force that acts on it towards the equilibrium position
How does the restoring force on an object in SHM depend on its displacement? | It is directly proportional to the displacement, in the opposite direction
How does the period of an object in SHM depend on its amplitude? | It does not. The period is the same for any amplitude
Explain why a person bouncing on a trampoline is not in simple harmonic motion | While they are in the air the only force on them is their weight, which is constant and so not proportional to displacement
What does a graph of acceleration against displacement look like for SHM? | A straight line through the origin with a negative gradient
What is the gradient of a graph of acceleration against displacement for SHM? | $-\omega^2$
What is the acceleration of an oscillator with $\omega = 4.0\;\text{rad s}^{-1}$ at a displacement of 0.050 m? | $a = -\omega^2 x = -4.0^2 \times 0.050 = -0.80\;\text{m s}^{-2}$
What is the equation for the displacement of an object in SHM released from its maximum displacement at $t = 0$? | $x = A\cos\omega t$
What is the equation for the displacement of an object in SHM that passes through its equilibrium position at $t = 0$? | $x = A\sin\omega t$
An oscillator of amplitude 4.0 cm and $\omega = 2.0\;\text{rad s}^{-1}$ is released from its maximum displacement. What is its displacement after 1.0 s? | $x = A\cos\omega t = 4.0 \times \cos(2.0 \times 1.0) = -1.7\;\text{cm}$
What is the equation for the velocity of an object in SHM at displacement $x$? | $v = \pm\omega\sqrt{A^2 - x^2}$
What does the $\pm$ sign in $v = \pm\omega\sqrt{A^2 - x^2}$ show? | At any displacement the object may be moving in either direction
What is the speed of an oscillator with $\omega = 5.0\;\text{rad s}^{-1}$ and amplitude 0.10 m at a displacement of 0.060 m? | $v = \omega\sqrt{A^2 - x^2} = 5.0 \times \sqrt{0.10^2 - 0.060^2} = 0.40\;\text{m s}^{-1}$
What is the equation for the maximum speed of an object in SHM? | $v_{\text{max}} = \omega A$
Where in its oscillation does an object in SHM have its maximum speed? | At the equilibrium position, $x = 0$
Where in its oscillation is the speed of an object in SHM zero? | At its maximum displacement, $x = \pm A$
What is the maximum speed of an oscillator with $\omega = 3.0\;\text{rad s}^{-1}$ and amplitude 0.20 m? | $v_{\text{max}} = \omega A = 3.0 \times 0.20 = 0.60\;\text{m s}^{-1}$
What is the equation for the maximum acceleration of an object in SHM? | $a_{\text{max}} = \omega^2 A$
Where in its oscillation does an object in SHM have its maximum acceleration? | At its maximum displacement, $x = \pm A$
Where in its oscillation is the acceleration of an object in SHM zero? | At the equilibrium position
What is the maximum acceleration of an oscillator with $\omega = 3.0\;\text{rad s}^{-1}$ and amplitude 0.20 m? | $a_{\text{max}} = \omega^2 A = 3.0^2 \times 0.20 = 1.8\;\text{m s}^{-2}$
How is the velocity-time graph of an oscillator found from its displacement-time graph? | From the gradient of the displacement-time graph
How is the acceleration-time graph of an oscillator found from its velocity-time graph? | From the gradient of the velocity-time graph
What is the phase difference between the displacement and the velocity of an object in SHM? | $\frac{\pi}{2}$ rad, a quarter of a cycle
What is the phase difference between the displacement and the acceleration of an object in SHM? | $\pi$ rad. They are in antiphase
For an oscillator released from its maximum positive displacement, what shapes are its displacement-time, velocity-time and acceleration-time graphs? | Displacement: cosine. Velocity: negative sine. Acceleration: negative cosine
What is the equation for the period of a mass-spring system? | $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{m}{k}}$
What is the period of a 0.50 kg mass oscillating on a spring of spring constant $20\;\text{N m}^{-1}$? | $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{m}{k}} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{0.50}{20}} = 0.99\;\text{s}$
What is the restoring force on the mass in a mass-spring system at displacement $x$ from equilibrium? | $F = -kx$
How does the period of a mass-spring system change when the mass is made four times larger? | It doubles, since $T \propto \sqrt{m}$
How does a stiffer spring affect the period of a mass-spring system? | The period is shorter, since $T \propto \frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}$
How would the period of a mass-spring system on the Moon compare with its period on Earth? | It would be the same, because the period does not depend on $g$
What is the equation for the period of a simple pendulum? | $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{l}{g}}$
In $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{l}{g}}$, what length is $l$? | The distance from the point of suspension to the centre of mass of the bob
What is the period of a simple pendulum of length 1.0 m where $g = 9.81\;\text{m s}^{-2}$? | $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{l}{g}} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{1.0}{9.81}} = 2.0\;\text{s}$
How does the mass of the bob affect the period of a simple pendulum? | It has no effect
How does the period of a simple pendulum change when its length is made four times larger? | It doubles, since $T \propto \sqrt{l}$
How would the period of a simple pendulum on the Moon compare with its period on Earth? | It would be longer, because $g$ is smaller
For what size of swing does a simple pendulum move with simple harmonic motion? | Small angles only, less than about $10^\circ$ from the vertical
What approximation is used to derive the period of a simple pendulum? | The small-angle approximation, $\sin\theta \approx \theta$ with $\theta$ in radians
What provides the restoring force on the bob of a simple pendulum? | The component of its weight along the arc, towards the equilibrium position
What is assumed about the string of a simple pendulum? | It is light and inextensible
Between which two forms does the energy of a mass oscillating horizontally on a spring change? | Kinetic energy and elastic potential energy
Between which two forms does the energy of a simple pendulum change? | Kinetic energy and gravitational potential energy
Where in its oscillation is the kinetic energy of an object in SHM greatest? | At the equilibrium position
Where in its oscillation is the potential energy of an object in SHM greatest? | At its maximum displacement
How does the total energy of an undamped oscillator vary with time? | It stays constant, equal to the sum of its kinetic and potential energies
What is the total energy of an oscillator of mass $m$ in SHM in terms of its amplitude? | $E = \frac{1}{2}m\omega^2 A^2$, its kinetic energy at the equilibrium position
What shape is a graph of potential energy against displacement for SHM? | A U-shaped curve that is zero at the equilibrium position and greatest at $x = \pm A$
What shape is a graph of kinetic energy against displacement for SHM? | An inverted U-shaped curve that is greatest at the equilibrium position and zero at $x = \pm A$
How many times does the kinetic energy of an object in SHM reach its maximum in one oscillation? | Twice
What is found from the mass-spring part of the simple harmonic motion required practical? | How the period depends on the mass, and from it the spring constant
What graph is plotted for a mass-spring system in the simple harmonic motion practical, and what shape is it? | $T^2$ against $m$, a straight line through the origin
What is the gradient of a graph of $T^2$ against $m$ for a mass-spring system? | $\frac{4\pi^2}{k}$
What is found from the pendulum part of the simple harmonic motion required practical? | How the period depends on the length, and from it a value for $g$
What graph is plotted for a simple pendulum in the simple harmonic motion practical, and what shape is it? | $T^2$ against $l$, a straight line through the origin
What is the gradient of a graph of $T^2$ against $l$ for a simple pendulum? | $\frac{4\pi^2}{g}$
A graph of $T^2$ against $l$ for a simple pendulum has a gradient of $4.0\;\text{s}^2\,\text{m}^{-1}$. What value of $g$ does it give? | $g = \frac{4\pi^2}{\text{gradient}} = \frac{4\pi^2}{4.0} = 9.9\;\text{m s}^{-2}$
Why is the time for ten or more oscillations measured when finding the period? | It reduces the percentage uncertainty in the period, because the reaction-time error is shared between many oscillations
What is a fiducial marker used for when timing oscillations? | It marks the equilibrium position, so timing starts and stops as the oscillator passes a fixed point
Why are oscillations timed as the oscillator passes its equilibrium position? | It is moving fastest there, so the moment it passes can be judged most precisely
Why must a pendulum be released from a small angle in the simple harmonic motion practical? | The equation for its period only holds for small angles
How can the timing of oscillations be made more accurate than with a stopwatch? | Use a light gate or motion sensor with a data logger, which removes the reaction-time error
