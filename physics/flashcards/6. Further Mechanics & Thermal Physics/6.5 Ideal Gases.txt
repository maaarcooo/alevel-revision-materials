What is absolute zero? | The lowest possible temperature, at which molecules have minimum kinetic energy
The lowest possible temperature, at which molecules have minimum kinetic energy - what term is this? | Absolute zero
What is absolute zero in kelvin and in degrees Celsius? | $0\ \text{K}$ and $-273.15\ ^\circ\text{C}$
How much more energy can be removed from a system at 0 K? | None

What two quantities are assumed constant for all three ideal gas laws? | The mass and the number of molecules of the gas
State Boyle's law | For a fixed mass of gas at constant temperature, pressure is inversely proportional to volume ($p \propto \frac{1}{V}$)
How is Boyle's law written in terms of initial and final states? | $p_1V_1 = p_2V_2$
A fixed mass of gas at constant temperature has a pressure of $1.0 \times 10^5\ \text{Pa}$ at a volume of $3.0 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{m}^3$. Find its pressure at $1.5 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{m}^3$ | $p_2 = \frac{p_1V_1}{V_2} = \frac{(1.0 \times 10^5)(3.0 \times 10^{-3})}{1.5 \times 10^{-3}} = 2.0 \times 10^5\ \text{Pa}$
What shape is a graph of volume against pressure for a fixed mass of gas at constant temperature? | A curve showing inverse proportionality, approaching both axes
How does a Boyle's law curve change if the constant temperature is higher? | Same shape, shifted further from the origin
State Charles's law | For a fixed mass of gas at constant pressure, volume is directly proportional to absolute temperature ($V \propto T$)
How is Charles's law written in terms of initial and final states? | $\frac{V_1}{T_1} = \frac{V_2}{T_2}$
A fixed mass of gas at constant pressure has a volume of $2.0 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{m}^3$ at 300 K. Find its volume at 450 K | $V_2 = \frac{V_1T_2}{T_1} = \frac{(2.0 \times 10^{-3})(450)}{300} = 3.0 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{m}^3$
State the pressure law | For a fixed mass of gas at constant volume, pressure is directly proportional to absolute temperature ($p \propto T$)
How is the pressure law written in terms of initial and final states? | $\frac{p_1}{T_1} = \frac{p_2}{T_2}$
What graph shape shows direct proportionality, as in Charles's law and the pressure law plotted against temperature in kelvin? | A straight line through the origin
In what unit must temperature always be when using any ideal gas law? | Kelvin (K)
On a graph of gas volume against temperature in °C at constant pressure, where does the extrapolated line meet the temperature axis? | At $-273\ ^\circ\text{C}$ (absolute zero)

What single relation combines the three gas laws for a fixed mass of ideal gas? | $pV \propto T$
What molecular property is the temperature of a gas related to? | The average kinetic energy of its molecules
Explain why the pressure of a gas rises when it is heated at constant volume | Molecules move faster, so they hit the walls more frequently and with a greater change in momentum, increasing the force per unit area
Explain why the pressure of a gas rises when its volume is reduced at constant temperature | Molecules collide with each unit area of wall more frequently, increasing the force per unit area
Explain why a gas expands when heated at constant pressure | Molecules move faster and collide harder and more often with the walls. The gas must expand so the force per unit area stays the same
In an ideal gas, what is the volume of the molecules? | Negligible
What type of collisions occur between ideal gas molecules? | Elastic collisions
What forces act between the molecules of an ideal gas? | None, except during collisions
What change of state can an ideal gas never undergo? | It cannot be liquefied
Which three laws does an ideal gas obey? | Boyle's law, Charles's law and the pressure law
Define an ideal gas | A gas that obeys the equation of state $pV = nRT$ at all pressures, volumes and temperatures
A gas that obeys the equation of state $pV = nRT$ at all pressures, volumes and temperatures - what term is this? | An ideal gas
What is the ideal gas equation in terms of number of moles? | $pV = nRT$

What is the ideal gas equation in terms of number of molecules? | $pV = NkT$

What is the equation for the work done when a gas changes volume at constant pressure? | $W = p\Delta V$
How does the work done differ between a gas expanding and a gas being compressed? | Expanding: work is done by the gas. Compressed: work is done on the gas
A piston of cross-sectional area $A$ is pushed a distance $s$ by an expanding gas. What is the change in volume? | $\Delta V = As$
A gas expands by $0.015\ \text{m}^3$ at a constant $1.0 \times 10^5\ \text{Pa}$. Find the work done | $W = p\Delta V = (1.0 \times 10^5)(0.015) = 1500\ \text{J}$
What is the atomic mass unit (u)? | Approximately the mass of a proton or neutron, $1.66 \times 10^{-27}\ \text{kg}$

The number of atoms in 12 g of carbon-12 - what constant is this? | The Avogadro constant ($N_A$)

What is the mass of one mole of an element? | Its relative atomic mass in grams
What is molar mass? | The mass in grams of one mole of a substance, unit $\text{g mol}^{-1}$
How is the molar mass of a compound found? | Add the relative atomic masses of its atoms (e.g. $\text{H}_2\text{O}$: $2 \times 1 + 16 = 18\ \text{g mol}^{-1}$)
What is the equation for number of moles from mass? | $n = \frac{\text{mass}}{\text{molar mass}}$

What is the equation linking $k$, $R$ and $N_A$? | $k = \frac{R}{N_A}$
What does the Boltzmann constant relate? | Microscopic properties of particles (e.g. molecular kinetic energy) to macroscopic properties (e.g. temperature)
