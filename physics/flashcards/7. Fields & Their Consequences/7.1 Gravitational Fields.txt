What is a force field? | A region in which a body experiences a non-contact force
A region in which a body experiences a non-contact force — what term is this? | Force field
What is a non-contact force? | A force that acts between bodies without physical contact
What property must a particle have to experience a force in a gravitational field? | Mass
Which type of force field acts on a charged particle whether it is stationary or moving? | Electric field
What must a charged particle be doing to experience a force in a magnetic field? | Moving across the field lines
What does the direction of a field line show? | The direction of the force on a body placed at that point in the field
Give an effect on Earth of the Moon's and Sun's gravitational fields | Tides
What is a gravitational field? | A region of space where a mass experiences a force due to the gravitational attraction of another mass
What is the weight of an object? | The gravitational force acting on it
Gravitational forces between masses are always [...] | attractive
What is the range of the gravitational force? | Infinite
What is gravitational field strength at a point? | The gravitational force per unit mass on a mass placed at that point ($g = \frac{F}{m}$)
The gravitational force per unit mass on a mass placed at a point — what quantity is this? | Gravitational field strength, $g$
What is the unit of gravitational field strength? | $\text{N kg}^{-1}$ (equivalent to $\text{m s}^{-2}$)
What is the gravitational field strength where a 2.0 kg mass experiences a gravitational force of 19.6 N? | $g = \frac{F}{m} = \frac{19.6}{2.0} = 9.8\;\text{N kg}^{-1}$
What type of quantity is gravitational field strength, and in what direction does it act? | A vector, directed towards the centre of mass of the body creating the field
What is $g$ equal to for an object falling freely in a gravitational field? | The object's acceleration
Which of mass and weight changes when an object is taken to a planet with a different $g$? | Weight. Mass stays the same everywhere
Which two properties of a planet determine the gravitational field strength at its surface? | Its mass and its radius
How does $g$ differ from $G$? | $g$ is gravitational field strength, which varies with position. $G$ is the universal gravitational constant
What is the pattern of gravitational field lines around a point mass? | Radial lines pointing inwards towards the mass
How are the field lines of a uniform gravitational field drawn? | Parallel, equally spaced lines
Where is Earth's gravitational field approximately uniform? | Near its surface
How does $g$ vary in a uniform field compared with a radial field? | Uniform: $g$ is the same at every point. Radial: $g$ decreases with distance from the centre
When can a uniform sphere be treated as a point mass at its centre? | For any point outside the sphere
What is a uniform sphere? | A sphere whose mass is distributed evenly
Why can stars and planets be treated as point masses when applying Newton's law of gravitation? | They are approximately uniform spheres and their separation is much larger than their radii
State Newton's law of gravitation | The gravitational force between two point masses is proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of their separation
What is the equation for Newton's law of gravitation? | $F = \frac{Gm_1m_2}{r^2}$
What is the gravitational force between two 1000 kg point masses 2.0 m apart? ($G = 6.67 \times 10^{-11}\;\text{N m}^2\,\text{kg}^{-2}$) | $F = \frac{Gm_1m_2}{r^2} = \frac{6.67 \times 10^{-11} \times 1000 \times 1000}{2.0^2} = 1.7 \times 10^{-5}\;\text{N}$
The $\frac{1}{r^2}$ relationship in gravitation is called the [...] | inverse square law
What happens to the gravitational force between two point masses when their separation doubles? | It falls to a quarter of its original value
From where is the distance $r$ measured in gravitational equations? | From the centre of mass of each body
What is $r$ for a satellite orbiting at height $h$ above a planet of radius $R$? | $r = R + h$
What is the equation for gravitational field strength due to a point mass $M$? | $g = \frac{GM}{r^2}$
What is the gravitational field strength at the surface of a planet of mass $6.0 \times 10^{24}$ kg and radius $6.4 \times 10^{6}$ m? ($G = 6.67 \times 10^{-11}\;\text{N m}^2\,\text{kg}^{-2}$) | $g = \frac{GM}{r^2} = \frac{6.67 \times 10^{-11} \times 6.0 \times 10^{24}}{(6.4 \times 10^{6})^2} = 9.8\;\text{N kg}^{-1}$
How does $g$ vary with distance $r$ from the centre of a planet of radius $R$, for $r > R$? | $g \propto \frac{1}{r^2}$
How does $g$ vary with distance $r$ from the centre inside a planet of uniform density ($r < R$)? | $g \propto r$
At what distance from the centre of a uniform-density planet is $g$ greatest? | At the surface ($r = R$)
A planet has surface field strength $g$ and radius $R$. What is the field strength at a distance $3R$ from its centre? | $\frac{g}{9}$
What is the surface gravitational field strength of a uniform planet in terms of its density $\rho$ and radius $R$? | $g = \frac{4}{3}\pi G\rho R$
