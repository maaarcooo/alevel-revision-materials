What provides the centripetal force on a planet in a circular orbit around the Sun? | The gravitational force between the Sun and the planet
In a circular orbit, what is the direction of the gravitational force on a satellite relative to its velocity? | Perpendicular to the velocity, towards the centre of the orbit
Which two forces are equated to derive the speed of a satellite in a circular orbit? | Gravitational force and centripetal force: $\frac{GMm}{r^2} = \frac{mv^2}{r}$
What is the equation for the speed of a satellite in a circular orbit of radius $r$? | $v^2 = \frac{GM}{r}$, where $M$ is the mass of the body being orbited
Why does the mass of a satellite not affect its orbital speed at a given radius? | The satellite's mass cancels from both sides of the force equation
What is the speed of a satellite in a circular orbit of radius $7.0 \times 10^6$ m around a planet of mass $6.0 \times 10^{24}$ kg? ($G = 6.67 \times 10^{-11}\ \text{N m}^2\,\text{kg}^{-2}$) | $v = \sqrt{\frac{GM}{r}} = \sqrt{\frac{6.67 \times 10^{-11} \times 6.0 \times 10^{24}}{7.0 \times 10^6}} = 7.6 \times 10^3\ \text{m s}^{-1}$
How does orbital speed vary with orbital radius for satellites orbiting the same body? | Speed decreases as radius increases ($v \propto \frac{1}{\sqrt{r}}$)
Why does a satellite moving at constant speed in a circular orbit have an acceleration? | Its direction of motion is constantly changing, so its velocity changes (centripetal acceleration)
What is the linear speed of an orbiting satellite in terms of its orbital radius $r$ and period $T$? | $v = \frac{2\pi r}{T}$
What equation relates the period $T$ of a circular orbit to its radius $r$? | $T^2 = \frac{4\pi^2 r^3}{GM}$
What does Kepler's third law state? | For orbits around the same central body, the square of the orbital period is proportional to the cube of the orbital radius ($T^2 \propto r^3$)
What is the period of a satellite in a circular orbit of radius $4.2 \times 10^7$ m around a planet of mass $6.0 \times 10^{24}$ kg? ($G = 6.67 \times 10^{-11}\ \text{N m}^2\,\text{kg}^{-2}$) | $T = \sqrt{\frac{4\pi^2 r^3}{GM}} = \sqrt{\frac{4\pi^2 \times (4.2 \times 10^7)^3}{6.67 \times 10^{-11} \times 6.0 \times 10^{24}}} = 8.5 \times 10^4\ \text{s}$
For orbits around one central body, what graph of $T$ and $r$ gives a straight line through the origin? | $T^2$ against $r^3$
What is the gradient of a graph of $\log T$ against $\log r$ for orbits around the same central body? | $1.5$
In a binary star system, what distance is used in the gravitational force between the two stars? | Their separation, $R_1 + R_2$, not either star's own orbital radius
In a binary star system, which two quantities are the same for both stars? | Angular speed and orbital period
What happens to the kinetic and gravitational potential energy of a satellite as it travels around a circular orbit? | Both remain constant
What happens to a satellite's kinetic energy when it moves to a circular orbit of larger radius? | It decreases
What happens to a satellite's gravitational potential energy when it moves to a circular orbit of larger radius? | It increases (becomes less negative)
How does the gravitational force on a satellite change when it moves to a circular orbit of larger radius? | It decreases ($F \propto \frac{1}{r^2}$)
What is escape velocity? | The minimum speed an object needs to escape a gravitational field from a given point, with no further energy input
The minimum speed an object needs to escape a gravitational field from a given point, with no further energy input — what term is this? | Escape velocity
What is the escape velocity from a point a distance $r$ from the centre of a body of mass $M$? | $v = \sqrt{\frac{2GM}{r}}$
What energy equation is used to derive escape velocity? | $\frac{1}{2}mv^2 = \frac{GMm}{r}$: the kinetic energy at launch equals the energy needed to move the object from $r$ to infinity
Why is the escape velocity from Earth the same for a rocket and a tennis ball? | The escaping object's mass cancels from the energy equation
Which two properties of a planet determine the escape velocity from its surface? | Its mass and its radius
What is the escape velocity from the surface of a planet of mass $6.0 \times 10^{24}$ kg and radius $6.4 \times 10^6$ m? ($G = 6.67 \times 10^{-11}\ \text{N m}^2\,\text{kg}^{-2}$) | $v = \sqrt{\frac{2GM}{r}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \times 6.67 \times 10^{-11} \times 6.0 \times 10^{24}}{6.4 \times 10^6}} = 1.1 \times 10^4\ \text{m s}^{-1}$
Why do rockets not need to reach escape velocity to get into Earth orbit? | Their engines continuously supply energy through thrust
How does the energy needed to put an object into Earth orbit compare with the energy needed for it to escape Earth's gravitational field? | Less energy is needed to reach orbit
What is a synchronous orbit? | An orbit whose period equals the rotational period (length of day) of the body being orbited
An orbit whose period equals the rotational period (length of day) of the body being orbited — what term is this? | Synchronous orbit
What is a geosynchronous orbit? | A synchronous orbit around the Earth, with a period of 24 hours
What is a geostationary orbit? | A geosynchronous orbit in the plane of the equator, so the satellite stays above one fixed point on Earth's surface
A geosynchronous orbit in the plane of the equator, so the satellite stays above one fixed point on Earth's surface — what term is this? | Geostationary orbit
In which direction does a geostationary satellite orbit? | West to east, the same direction as Earth's rotation
Approximately what height above Earth's surface is a geostationary orbit? | About 36 000 km ($3.6 \times 10^7$ m)
How does a geosynchronous orbit differ from a geostationary orbit? | A geosynchronous orbit may be inclined or slightly elliptical, so the satellite does not stay above one point. A geostationary orbit is equatorial, so the satellite does
What are geostationary satellites used for? | Telecommunications and TV broadcasting
How is a TV signal transmitted via a geostationary satellite? | A ground station sends the signal up, and the satellite amplifies it and broadcasts it back down to receivers
Why can receiver dishes for geostationary satellites be fixed in position? | The satellite stays at the same point in the sky
What is a polar orbit? | A low orbit in which the satellite passes over the north and south poles
What are low orbits used for? | High-quality imaging of Earth's surface, such as for weather monitoring and military applications
