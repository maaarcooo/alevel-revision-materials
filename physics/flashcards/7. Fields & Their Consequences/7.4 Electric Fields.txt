State Coulomb's law | The electrostatic force between two point charges is directly proportional to the product of the charges and inversely proportional to the square of their separation
What is the equation for Coulomb's law? | $F = \frac{Q_1 Q_2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r^2}$
In Coulomb's law, between which points is the separation $r$ measured? | The centres of the two charges
What does the symbol $\varepsilon_0$ represent? | The permittivity of free space
What approximation is made about air when calculating the force between charges in air? | Air is treated as a vacuum, so $\varepsilon_0$ is used
Coulomb's law for charges is analogous to which law for masses? | Newton's law of gravitation
In Coulomb's law, what does a positive value of $F$ indicate? | Repulsion between like charges
In Coulomb's law, what does a negative value of $F$ indicate? | Attraction between opposite charges
What is the electrostatic force between two charges of $1.0 \times 10^{-6}$ C separated by 0.10 m in a vacuum? | $F = \frac{Q_1 Q_2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r^2} = \frac{(1.0 \times 10^{-6})^2}{4\pi (8.85 \times 10^{-12})(0.10)^2} = 0.90\ \text{N}$
Outside a uniformly charged sphere, where can the sphere's charge be considered to act? | At its centre, as a point charge
What is the electric field strength inside a charged conducting sphere? | Zero
What is an electric field? | A region of space in which a charged object experiences a force
Define electric field strength | The force per unit charge experienced by a small positive test charge placed at that point
The force per unit charge experienced by a small positive test charge placed at a point - what quantity is this? | Electric field strength
What is the equation for electric field strength in terms of force and charge? | $E = \frac{F}{Q}$
What is the unit of electric field strength? | $\text{N C}^{-1}$, equivalent to $\text{V m}^{-1}$
What type of quantity is electric field strength? | A vector
Why does the definition of electric field strength specify a positive test charge? | It sets the convention for the direction of the field
In what direction is the force on a positive charge in an electric field? | In the direction of the field
In what direction is the force on a negative charge in an electric field? | Opposite to the direction of the field
What is the electric field strength where a 2.0 μC charge experiences a force of 0.050 N? | $E = \frac{F}{Q} = \frac{0.050}{2.0 \times 10^{-6}} = 2.5 \times 10^4\ \text{N C}^{-1}$
Which two properties of an electric field do field lines represent? | Its direction and its magnitude
In what direction do electric field lines point between charges? | From the positive charge to the negative charge
How does the spacing of electric field lines indicate field strength? | Closer lines mean a stronger field
What do the field lines of a uniform electric field look like? | Parallel and equally spaced
How does electric field strength vary across a uniform field? | It is constant in magnitude and direction at all points
What is the field around a positive point charge like? | Radial, pointing outwards
What is the field around a negative point charge like? | Radial, pointing inwards
How do the field lines between two opposite charges differ from those between two like charges? | Opposite charges: lines run from one charge to the other. Like charges: lines do not connect the two charges
Where is the neutral point between two equal like charges? | At the midpoint, where the resultant field is zero
What two rules apply when sketching electric field lines? | Lines never cross, and they start or end on the surface of a charge or plate
What type of field is produced between two oppositely charged parallel plates? | A uniform field
What is the electric field like beyond the edges of two charged parallel plates? | Weaker and non-uniform
What is the equation for the electric field strength between two parallel plates? | $E = \frac{V}{d}$, where $V$ is the potential difference and $d$ is the plate separation
What is the electric field strength between parallel plates 0.020 m apart with a potential difference of 500 V? | $E = \frac{V}{d} = \frac{500}{0.020} = 2.5 \times 10^4\ \text{V m}^{-1}$
Why can $E = \frac{V}{d}$ not be used for the field around a point charge? | The field around a point charge is radial, not uniform
What is the potential of an earthed plate? | 0 V
How is $E = \frac{V}{d}$ derived from the work done moving a charge $Q$ between parallel plates? | The work done is $Fd = Q\Delta V$, so $\frac{F}{Q} = \frac{\Delta V}{d}$, and $\frac{F}{Q}$ is $E$
How does a charged particle initially at rest in a uniform electric field move? | It accelerates in a straight line parallel to the field lines
What path does a charged particle follow when it enters a uniform electric field at right angles to the field lines? | A parabola
Why does a charged particle entering a uniform field at right angles follow a parabolic path? | It experiences a constant force in a fixed direction perpendicular to its initial velocity, like a projectile
Towards which plate is a positive particle deflected between charged parallel plates? | The negative plate
Towards which plate is a negative particle deflected between charged parallel plates? | The positive plate
How does a neutron move through the field between charged parallel plates? | In a straight line, undeflected, because it has no charge
For a charged particle entering a uniform field at right angles, how does a greater mass affect its deflection? | Deflection decreases
For a charged particle entering a uniform field at right angles, how does a greater charge affect its deflection? | Deflection increases
For a charged particle entering a uniform field at right angles, how does a greater speed affect its deflection? | Deflection decreases
Explain why a boron-11 nucleus is deflected less than a proton entering the same uniform field with the same velocity | Its charge is 5 times larger but its mass is 11 times larger, so its acceleration towards the plate is smaller
What is the equation for the electric field strength at distance $r$ from a point charge $Q$? | $E = \frac{Q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r^2}$
How does the electric field strength of a point charge vary with distance $r$? | Inverse square law, $E \propto \frac{1}{r^2}$
What is the electric field strength 0.30 m from a point charge of $1.0 \times 10^{-9}$ C? | $E = \frac{Q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r^2} = \frac{1.0 \times 10^{-9}}{4\pi (8.85 \times 10^{-12})(0.30)^2} = 1.0 \times 10^2\ \text{N C}^{-1}$
How is the resultant electric field at a point due to several charges found? | By vector addition of the individual fields
What is the magnitude of the resultant of two perpendicular electric fields $E_1$ and $E_2$? | $\sqrt{E_1^2 + E_2^2}$
What is the resultant field at a point where perpendicular fields of $3.0\;\text{kN C}^{-1}$ and $4.0\;\text{kN C}^{-1}$ act? | $\sqrt{3.0^2 + 4.0^2} = 5.0\ \text{kN C}^{-1}$
How does the origin of the gravitational force differ from that of the electrostatic force? | Gravitational force acts between masses. Electrostatic force acts between charges
How does the nature of the gravitational force differ from that of the electrostatic force? | Gravitational force is always attractive. Electrostatic force can be attractive or repulsive
How does the gravitational force between two protons compare with the electrostatic force between them? | It is negligibly small in comparison
The field lines around a point mass are identical to those around which type of point charge? | A negative point charge
How do gravitational and electric potential vary with distance $r$ from a point mass or charge? | Both are proportional to $\frac{1}{r}$
