When a capacitor charges, what happens to the plate connected to the positive terminal of the supply? | Electrons are pulled off it, leaving it positively charged
When a capacitor charges, where do the electrons removed from one plate go? | Around the circuit onto the plate connected to the negative terminal, making it negatively charged
Explain why the charging current of a capacitor decreases as it charges | Electrons already on the negative plate repel incoming electrons, so fewer are pushed onto it as charge builds up
When does a capacitor connected to a supply stop charging? | When the PD across the capacitor equals the PD of the supply
How does the current vary with time while a capacitor charges? | It starts at a maximum and decreases exponentially towards zero
How do the charge and PD of a charging capacitor vary with time? | They rise from zero, quickly at first then more slowly, towards a maximum value
How do the shapes of the charge-time and PD-time graphs for a charging capacitor compare? | They are identical
What is meant by exponential decay of a quantity? | Its rate of decrease is proportional to the amount of the quantity remaining
How is a charged capacitor discharged? | Through a resistor, with no power supply in the circuit
In which direction do electrons flow when a capacitor discharges through a resistor? | From the negative plate back to the positive plate, until there is no PD between the plates
How does the direction of the discharge current compare with the charging current? | It is in the opposite direction
How do current, PD and charge vary with time for a capacitor discharging through a resistor? | All three decrease exponentially from their initial values, with identically shaped graphs
How does increasing the resistance affect the discharge of a capacitor? | Charge flows off the plates more slowly, so the capacitor takes longer to discharge
For a capacitor discharging through a resistor, how is the current related to the PD across the capacitor and the charge on it? | Directly proportional to both
What does the area under a current-time graph for a charging or discharging capacitor represent? | The charge that flows onto or off the plates in that time interval
What does the gradient of a charge-time graph for a capacitor represent? | The current at that time
How is the current found from a curved charge-time graph at a given time? | Draw a tangent to the curve at that time and calculate its gradient
What is the time constant of a capacitor discharging through a resistor? | The time for the charge, current or PD to fall to $\frac{1}{e}$ (about 37%) of its initial value
The time for the charge, current or PD of a discharging capacitor to fall to $\frac{1}{e}$ (about 37%) of its initial value - what term is this? | Time constant
What is the time constant of a capacitor charging through a resistor? | The time for the charge or PD to rise to about 63% of its maximum value
What value is the time constant measured relative to for a charging capacitor compared with a discharging one? | Charging: the maximum (final) value. Discharging: the initial value
What is the equation for the time constant of a capacitor-resistor circuit? | Time constant $= RC$, the resistance multiplied by the capacitance
What is the unit of the time constant? | Second (s)
What is the time constant of a $2\;\mu\text{F}$ capacitor discharging through a $5\;\text{k}\Omega$ resistor? | $RC = 5000 \times 2 \times 10^{-6} = 0.01\;\text{s}$
How does a smaller time constant affect the discharge of a capacitor? | The exponential decay is quicker
How is the time constant found from a PD-time graph for a discharging capacitor? | Read off the time at which $V = 0.37V_0$
How is the time constant found from a PD-time graph for a charging capacitor? | Read off the time at which $V = 0.63V_0$
What is the time to halve, $T_{1/2}$, of a discharging capacitor? | The time for the charge, current or PD to fall to half its initial value
The time for the charge, current or PD of a discharging capacitor to fall to half its initial value - what term is this? | Time to halve ($T_{1/2}$)
What is the equation for the time to halve of a discharging capacitor? | $T_{1/2} = 0.69RC$
What is the time to halve of a capacitor discharging with a time constant of 10 s? | $T_{1/2} = 0.69RC = 0.69 \times 10 = 6.9\;\text{s}$
How does the time to halve of a discharging capacitor depend on its initial charge? | It is independent of it, so the charge always takes the same time to halve
What is the equation for the charge on a capacitor discharging through a resistor? | $Q = Q_0 e^{-\frac{t}{RC}}$
What is the equation for the PD across a capacitor discharging through a resistor? | $V = V_0 e^{-\frac{t}{RC}}$
What is the equation for the current from a capacitor discharging through a resistor? | $I = I_0 e^{-\frac{t}{RC}}$
A capacitor with initial charge $50\;\mu\text{C}$ discharges with $RC = 4\;\text{s}$. What is the charge after 4 s? | $Q = Q_0 e^{-\frac{t}{RC}} = 50 \times e^{-1} = 18\;\mu\text{C}$
What is the equation for the charge on a capacitor charging through a resistor? | $Q = Q_0\left(1 - e^{-\frac{t}{RC}}\right)$
What is the equation for the PD across a capacitor charging through a resistor? | $V = V_0\left(1 - e^{-\frac{t}{RC}}\right)$
What is the equation for the current while a capacitor charges through a resistor? | $I = I_0 e^{-\frac{t}{RC}}$, the same form as for discharge
How does the meaning of $Q_0$ and $V_0$ differ between the charging and discharging equations? | Charging: the final (maximum) charge and PD. Discharging: the initial charge and PD
A capacitor charges towards a maximum of 12 V with $RC = 2\;\text{s}$. What is the PD across it after 2 s? | $V = V_0\left(1 - e^{-\frac{t}{RC}}\right) = 12\left(1 - e^{-1}\right) = 7.6\;\text{V}$

Why is a large resistance used in the capacitor discharge practical? | So the capacitor discharges slowly enough for readings to be timed accurately
