What is radioactive decay? | The spontaneous disintegration of an unstable nucleus to form a more stable nucleus, emitting alpha, beta or gamma radiation
What does it mean for radioactive decay to be random? | It is impossible to predict which nucleus will decay next or when a particular nucleus will decay
What does it mean for radioactive decay to be spontaneous? | It is not affected by external conditions such as temperature or pressure
How does the probability of decay per unit time compare between nuclei of the same isotope? | It is the same for every nucleus and stays constant over time
What can be predicted about the decay of a large sample of nuclei? | The proportion of nuclei expected to decay in a given time
What evidence from a GM tube shows that radioactive decay is random? | The count rate fluctuates irregularly and unpredictably
What is the decay constant $\lambda$? | The probability that an individual nucleus will decay per unit time
The probability that an individual nucleus will decay per unit time - what quantity is this? | The decay constant $\lambda$
What is the unit of the decay constant? | $\text{s}^{-1}$ (or any inverse time unit, e.g. $\text{year}^{-1}$)
What is the activity of a radioactive sample? | The number of nuclei that decay per unit time
The number of nuclei in a sample that decay per unit time - what quantity is this? | Activity
What is the unit of activity? | The becquerel (Bq), where 1 Bq = 1 decay per second
What is the equation for the rate of change of the number of undecayed nuclei $N$? | $\frac{\Delta N}{\Delta t} = -\lambda N$
What does the minus sign in $\frac{\Delta N}{\Delta t} = -\lambda N$ show? | The number of undecayed nuclei decreases with time
What is the equation linking activity, decay constant and number of undecayed nuclei? | $A = \lambda N$
What is the activity of a sample of $5.0 \times 10^{12}$ undecayed nuclei with $\lambda = 2.0 \times 10^{-5}\;\text{s}^{-1}$? | $A = \lambda N = 2.0 \times 10^{-5} \times 5.0 \times 10^{12} = 1.0 \times 10^{8}\;\text{Bq}$
What equation gives the number of undecayed nuclei $N$ remaining after time $t$? | $N = N_0 e^{-\lambda t}$
How many of $1.0 \times 10^{6}$ undecayed nuclei remain after 10 s if $\lambda = 0.10\;\text{s}^{-1}$? | $N = N_0 e^{-\lambda t} = 1.0 \times 10^{6} \times e^{-1.0} = 3.7 \times 10^{5}$
What equation gives the activity $A$ of a sample after time $t$? | $A = A_0 e^{-\lambda t}$
What equation gives the background-corrected count rate $C$ from a source after time $t$? | $C = C_0 e^{-\lambda t}$
How does the decay constant affect the shape of a graph of undecayed nuclei against time? | A larger $\lambda$ gives a steeper curve
How can dice model radioactive decay? | Each throw, remove the dice showing a chosen face. Every die has the same fixed chance of being removed each throw, like the decay probability of a nucleus
What is the decay constant of dice with $n$ faces, when one face counts as decayed? | $\lambda = \frac{1}{n}$ per throw
What is half-life? | The time taken for the number of undecayed nuclei of an isotope (or its activity) to halve
The time taken for the number of undecayed nuclei of an isotope (or its activity) to halve - what term is this? | Half-life
What is the equation linking half-life and decay constant? | $T_{1/2} = \frac{\ln 2}{\lambda}$
What is the half-life of an isotope with decay constant $1.0 \times 10^{-3}\;\text{s}^{-1}$? | $T_{1/2} = \frac{\ln 2}{\lambda} = \frac{0.693}{1.0 \times 10^{-3}} = 693\;\text{s}$
How does the half-life of an isotope change as its decay constant increases? | It decreases (half-life is inversely proportional to $\lambda$)
What fraction of the original undecayed nuclei remains after $n$ half-lives? | $\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^n$
How is half-life found from a graph of activity against time? | Read off the time taken for the activity to fall from $A_0$ to $\frac{A_0}{2}$
What graph of radioactive decay data gives a straight line? | $\ln N$ against $t$
What is the gradient of a graph of $\ln N$ against $t$ for a decaying sample? | $-\lambda$
What is the y-intercept of a graph of $\ln N$ against $t$ for a decaying sample? | $\ln N_0$
What is the equation for the number of nuclei in a sample of known mass? | $N = \frac{\text{mass} \times N_A}{\text{molar mass}}$
How many nuclei are in 2.0 g of a nuclide with molar mass $200\;\text{g mol}^{-1}$? | $N = \frac{\text{mass} \times N_A}{\text{molar mass}} = \frac{2.0 \times 6.02 \times 10^{23}}{200} = 6.02 \times 10^{21}$
Name three medical uses of radioactivity | Radiotherapy, tracers and sterilising equipment
Why is the proportion of carbon-14 in a living organism constant? | Carbon is continually taken in, replacing the carbon-14 that decays
Why does the carbon-14 activity of organic matter fall after the organism dies? | It no longer takes in carbon, so decayed carbon-14 is not replaced
How is the age of a once-living sample found by radiocarbon dating? | Compare its current carbon-14 activity with the initial value, estimated from the carbon-14 to carbon-12 ratio in living material
Why is radiocarbon dating unreliable for samples only a few hundred years old? | Too little carbon-14 has decayed, so the fall in activity is too small to measure accurately
Why is radiocarbon dating unreliable for samples tens of thousands of years old? | The activity is too low to distinguish from background radiation
What stable isotope does uranium-238 ultimately decay into? | Lead-206, via a decay chain
What is assumed about the lead-206 in a rock dated by uranium-lead dating? | None was present initially, so all of it came from uranium-238 decay
How is the age of a rock found by uranium-lead dating? | From the ratio of lead-206 to uranium-238 atoms, which increases with time
What does a radioisotope power system do? | Converts heat released by radioactive decay into electrical power
What are radioisotope power systems used to power? | Space probes and satellites
Which isotope is typically used as fuel in radioisotope power systems? | Plutonium-238 (an alpha emitter)
How does the power output of a radioisotope power system change over time? | It decreases exponentially, in proportion to the source's activity
