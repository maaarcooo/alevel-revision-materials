## Newton's Corpuscular Theory and Huygens' Wave Theory

### Corpuscular Theory

**Newton's corpuscular theory:** light is made of tiny particles, **corpuscles**, emitted by luminous objects. It predicted that objects that emit light slowly lose mass.

| Phenomenon | Corpuscular explanation |
|---|---|
| Straight-line travel and sharp shadows | Corpuscles travel in straight lines |
| Reflection | A repulsive force from the surface reverses the velocity component perpendicular to it and leaves the parallel component unchanged |
| Refraction towards the normal on entering a denser medium | A short-range attraction at the boundary increases the velocity component perpendicular to it, while the parallel component is unchanged. So light **speeds up** in the denser medium |

Corpuscular theory could **not** explain **diffraction** or **interference**.

### Wave Theory

**Huygens' theory:** light is a wave that travels as a series of wavefronts, through the **luminiferous aether**, a massless medium proposed to fill the Universe.

**Huygens' principle:** every point on a wavefront acts as a secondary source of wavelets, which combine to form the next wavefront.

| Phenomenon | Wave explanation |
|---|---|
| Reflection | Different parts of the wavefront reach the surface at different times, and each point of reflection emits new wavelets, so the new wavefront forms in a new direction |
| Refraction | The part of the wavefront that reaches the boundary first slows down before the rest, so the wavefront changes direction. So light **slows down** in the denser medium |

### Comparing the Theories

| | Corpuscular theory | Wave theory |
|---|---|---|
| Light is | Particles with mass | A wave in the aether |
| Reflection and refraction | Explained | Explained |
| Speed in a denser medium | Faster | Slower |
| Diffraction and interference | Not explained | Explained |

**Why Newton's theory was preferred** for over a century:

- Newton was already highly respected for his work on motion and gravity.
- The theories could not be tested against each other: the speed of light in a dense medium could not be measured, and diffraction of light had not been observed.

## Young's Double Slit Experiment

Young shone light through a single slit and then through two closely spaced slits. The screen showed **alternating bright and dark interference fringes**. Corpuscular theory predicts only two bright regions, one behind each slit.

- The **single slit** comes first so that light diffracting from it reaches both slits in phase. The two slits then act as **coherent sources**.
- **Explanation in wave terms:** wavelets from the two slits overlap. Bright fringes form where the path difference is a whole number of wavelengths, so the waves arrive in phase. Dark fringes form where it is an odd number of half wavelengths, so they arrive in antiphase.

The experiment provided evidence for **Huygens' wave theory**. It was not accepted at once, because of Newton's reputation and because corpuscular theory had been accepted for so long. Acceptance came after further evidence, including **Foucault's** measurement showing that light is **slower in water** than in air, as wave theory predicted.

## Electromagnetic Waves

### Maxwell's Prediction

Maxwell's equations predicted the existence of **electromagnetic waves**.

- An electromagnetic wave consists of oscillating **electric and magnetic fields**, **in phase**, **perpendicular** to each other and to the direction of travel.
- It is produced by an **accelerating charge**.
- It needs no medium: the changing electric field generates a changing magnetic field and vice versa, so the wave is **self-propagating**.

Maxwell's formula for the speed of electromagnetic waves in a vacuum is

$$c = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_0\varepsilon_0}}$$

| Constant | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $\mu_0$, permeability of free space | Relates magnetic flux density to the current producing it in free space |
| $\varepsilon_0$, permittivity of free space | Relates electric field strength to the charged object producing it in free space |

**Inline example:** $c = \frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi \times 10^{-7} \times 8.85 \times 10^{-12}}} = 3.00 \times 10^{8}\ \text{m s}^{-1}$, the measured speed of light, suggesting light is an electromagnetic wave.

### Hertz's Discovery of Radio Waves

**Transmitter:** a high voltage across a short air gap made sparks jump the gap, and the sparks emitted radio waves.

**Detectors:**

- a wire loop with a small gap, which showed it was receiving radio waves by sparks across the gap
- two parallel metal rods at the centre of a concave metal sheet, across which an oscillating pd was induced

| Test | Result | Shows |
|---|---|---|
| A metal screen placed behind the transmitter | Stronger signal at the detector | Radio waves are **reflected** |
| An insulator placed between transmitter and detector | No difference to the signal | Radio waves **pass through insulators** |
| Detector rotated through 90° about the line to the transmitter | The sparks stopped | Radio waves are **plane polarised**, because charges in the transmitter were accelerated in one direction only |

**Measuring the speed of radio waves:**

1. Reflect the waves off a flat metal sheet so the incident and reflected waves interfere, forming a **standing wave**.
2. Move the detector to locate the **antinodes**, where the signal is strongest. Adjacent antinodes are $\frac{\lambda}{2}$ apart, which gives the wavelength.
3. The frequency is known from the properties of the transmitter circuit.
4. Calculate $c = f\lambda$.

Hertz's measured speed matched the speed Maxwell predicted for electromagnetic waves, **confirming Maxwell's theory**.

### Fizeau's Determination of the Speed of Light

Before it was measured, the speed of light was believed to be infinite.

**Apparatus:** a light beam passes through a gap in a rapidly rotating **toothed wheel** to a mirror several kilometres away, then back through the wheel to an observer. The wheel chops the continuous beam into regular pulses.

At a low speed of rotation, light returns through the same gap. As the frequency $f$ increases, the observer **first sees no returning light** when light that passed through a gap returns to find the adjacent tooth in its place.

- Round-trip time for wheel-to-mirror distance $d$: $t = \frac{2d}{c}$.
- For a wheel with $n$ teeth rotating at frequency $f$, the time for a gap to be replaced by the adjacent tooth is $t = \frac{1}{2nf}$.
- Equating the two times gives

$$c = 4dnf$$

**Inline example:** a mirror 8.6 km away and a 720-tooth wheel first block the returning light at 12.6 revolutions per second, so $c = 4 \times 8600 \times 720 \times 12.6 = 3.1 \times 10^{8}\ \text{m s}^{-1}$.

If the first blocking happens at $f_0$, the returning light reappears at maximum brightness at $2f_0$, when it passes through the next gap, and is next blocked at $3f_0$, when it meets the following tooth.

**Implication:** Fizeau's speed matched Maxwell's predicted speed for electromagnetic waves, suggesting that light is an electromagnetic wave.

## The Discovery of Photoelectricity

### Black-Body Radiation

A **black body** is an object that absorbs all the radiation incident on it, reflecting and transmitting none. A good absorber is also a good emitter, so a perfect black body would also be the best possible emitter.

**Black-body radiation** is the electromagnetic radiation emitted by a black body. Its spectrum has a peak whose position depends only on the temperature.

- As temperature increases, the wavelength of peak intensity **decreases**.
- An object at room temperature emits most intensely in the **infrared**. Heated to a few thousand kelvin, it glows red, then orange, then white.

### The Ultraviolet Catastrophe

Classical wave theory predicted that the intensity of black-body radiation would **rise without limit** as wavelength decreased into the ultraviolet. This is the **ultraviolet catastrophe**.

Experimental curves showed a **peak**, with intensity falling to **zero** at short wavelengths. Classical theory agreed with experiment only at **long** wavelengths.

### Planck's Interpretation

Planck proposed that electromagnetic radiation is emitted in **quanta**, each with energy proportional to its frequency, $E = hf$. An oscillator of frequency $f$ can only have energies

$$E = nhf$$

where $n$ is an integer. With this assumption his theory matched the measured black-body curves.

### Failure of Classical Wave Theory

Classical wave theory treats light as transferring energy continuously, spread over the wavefront.

| Classical prediction | Observation |
|---|---|
| High-intensity radiation below the threshold frequency would eventually cause emission, since waves transfer energy continuously | No emission below the threshold frequency, however high the intensity |
| Weak radiation would cause emission after a delay, while electrons absorbed enough energy | Low-intensity radiation above the threshold frequency causes emission **immediately** |
| Greater intensity would give each electron more kinetic energy | Intensity changes only the **number** of photoelectrons. Their maximum kinetic energy depends on frequency |

### Einstein's Explanation

Einstein proposed that electromagnetic radiation consists of discrete packets of energy, **photons**, each of energy $E = hf$. This built on **Planck's** quantisation of energy in black-body radiation.

- Each photon transfers all its energy to one electron, in a one-to-one interaction.
- If $hf$ is less than the work function, no single photon can free an electron, however many arrive, so there is no emission below the threshold frequency.
- Above the threshold, emission is immediate, and the maximum kinetic energy is $hf - \phi$, which rises with frequency.
- Greater intensity means more photons per second, so more photoelectrons but no more energy each.

**Significance:** electromagnetic radiation behaves as **particles** (photons) when it interacts with matter, as well as a wave when it travels.

## Wave-Particle Duality

### De Broglie's Hypothesis

De Broglie proposed that if waves can behave as particles, then particles can behave as waves. A particle of momentum $p$ has a wavelength

$$\lambda = \frac{h}{p}$$

For an electron accelerated from rest through a pd $V$, $eV = \frac{p^2}{2m}$, so $p = \sqrt{2meV}$ and

$$\lambda = \frac{h}{\sqrt{2meV}}$$

The wavelength is inversely proportional to $\sqrt{V}$.

**Inline example:** if the accelerating pd is multiplied by 25, the de Broglie wavelength falls to $\frac{1}{\sqrt{25}} = \frac{1}{5}$ of its original value.

### Low-Energy Electron Diffraction

Electrons from an electron gun pass through a thin film of graphite in an evacuated tube. The regularly arranged carbon atoms diffract them, forming **rings** on a **fluorescent (phosphor) screen**, which glows where electrons strike. Diffraction is a wave property, so this confirmed that electrons behave as waves.

**Effect of electron speed:** raising the anode pd gives the electrons more speed and momentum, so a **shorter** wavelength, and the rings **shrink** towards the centre, as $\lambda = \frac{h}{p}$ predicts. Lowering the pd makes the rings larger.

## Electron Microscopes

**Resolving power** is a microscope's ability to distinguish between two nearby points on an object. A **shorter wavelength** gives **higher** resolving power, so finer detail. Electron microscopes can resolve more detail than light microscopes because electrons have a much shorter wavelength than visible light.

### Anode Voltage for Atomic-Scale Wavelengths

To resolve atoms, the electron wavelength must be of the order of the size of an atom, about $10^{-10}$ m. Rearranging $\lambda = \frac{h}{\sqrt{2meV}}$:

$$V = \frac{h^2}{2me\lambda^2}$$

**Inline example:** for $\lambda = 1.5 \times 10^{-10}$ m, $V = \frac{(6.63 \times 10^{-34})^2}{2 \times 9.11 \times 10^{-31} \times 1.60 \times 10^{-19} \times (1.5 \times 10^{-10})^2} = 67$ V.

### Transmission Electron Microscope (TEM)

An electron gun produces a beam of high-speed electrons, which passes **through** a thin sample. **Magnetic lenses** focus the beam, as glass lenses focus light.

| Lens | Function |
|---|---|
| Condenser lens | Deflects the electrons into a wide **parallel** beam that is uniformly incident on the sample |
| Objective lens | Forms an **image** of the sample |
| Projector lens | Casts a **magnified** second image onto the fluorescent screen |

**Limits on resolution:**

- Passing through the sample **slows** the electrons, so their wavelength increases and the resolution falls.
- The electrons have a **range of speeds**: they leave the gun at different speeds and are slowed by different amounts in the sample. Magnetic lenses deflect electrons of different speeds by different amounts, so electrons from one point on the sample land at a range of positions, blurring the image.

### Scanning Tunnelling Microscope (STM)

- The probe is a very fine **tip**, only a few atoms across.
- **Piezoelectric transducers** move the tip in tiny increments in any direction.
- The tip is held **very close** to the sample surface, at a **constant pd**.
- Electrons **tunnel** across the gap between the surface and the tip, producing a **tunnelling current**.
- The tunnelling current **increases** as the gap decreases.

**Quantum tunnelling:** the electrons lack the energy to cross the gap, which acts as a potential barrier, but their wave nature gives them a small probability of being found on the other side. This only happens if the barrier is **very narrow**.

| Mode | How it works | What is recorded to map the surface |
|---|---|---|
| Constant height | The tip stays at a fixed height, so the gap changes with the surface | Changes in tunnelling current |
| Constant current | The tip moves up or down to keep the gap, and so the current, the same | The vertical movement of the tip |

The STM has very high resolving power because the tunnelling current is very sensitive to changes in gap width. Its images can show **individual atoms** on a surface.
