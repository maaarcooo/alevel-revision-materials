## The Michelson-Morley Experiment

### The Aether and Absolute Motion

- The **luminiferous aether** was a hypothetical medium thought to fill space and carry light waves. Physicists assumed light needed a medium because all other known waves, such as sound and water waves, travel through one.
- **Absolute motion** is motion measured relative to a single fixed frame, the aether, that applies to every body in the Universe.
- The Earth moving through the aether would cause an apparent flow of aether past it, the **aether wind**.
- The **aim** of the Michelson-Morley experiment was to detect the Earth's absolute motion through the aether.

### The Interferometer

| Part | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Two arms of equal length at right angles, each with a mirror at its end | Each beam travels along one arm and back |
| **Beam splitter**: a semi-silvered mirror at the junction | Transmits part of the light and reflects the rest, splitting it into two beams |
| Extra glass plate in one arm | Makes both beams pass through the same thickness of glass and air |
| Apparatus floated on mercury | Lets it be rotated smoothly with minimal friction |

The two beams come from the same source, so they are **coherent**, and they form an **interference pattern** where they recombine at the eyepiece. Fringes are seen whatever the orientation, because the two beams always interfere.

### The Prediction

According to aether theory, a beam travelling along the arm **parallel** to the Earth's motion through the aether would take **longer** to travel there and back than a beam along the perpendicular arm. Rotating the interferometer by 90° swaps which arm is parallel to the aether wind, so the time difference between the beams would change and the **fringes would shift**.

Aether theory predicted a shift of about **0.4 of a fringe**. The apparatus could detect shifts of **0.05 of a fringe**.

### The Null Result and its Significance

The experiment gave a **null result**: no significant fringe shift at any orientation or time of day.

- **Absolute motion cannot be detected**, refuting the idea of a fixed aether frame.
- The speed of light is **invariant**: it is unaffected by the Earth's motion.
- A stationary observer measures the speed of light from a moving source as $c$, whatever the source's speed.

## Einstein's Theory of Special Relativity

### Frames of Reference

- A **frame of reference** is a set of coordinates used to record the position and time of events.
- An **inertial frame of reference** is a frame that is not accelerating, in which Newton's first law holds.
- Any two inertial frames move relative to each other at **constant velocity**.
- Special relativity applies only in inertial frames. It cannot be applied to a spacecraft whose path is bent by the Sun's gravity, because the spacecraft is accelerating.
- There is no absolutely stationary frame: **all motion is relative**.

**Galilean relativity:** Newton's laws are the same in all inertial frames, with space and time treated as absolute. Its rule for adding velocities fails near the speed of light, because it can give speeds above $c$: for example, $0.7c + 0.5c = 1.2c$, which is impossible.

### Einstein's Postulates

1. The laws of physics have the **same form** in all inertial frames of reference.
2. The **speed of light in free space** is the same in all inertial frames of reference.

**Consequences:**

- An experiment on a train moving at constant velocity gives identical results to the same experiment on the platform, so uniform motion cannot be detected.
- Two spaceships approach each other at $0.5c$ and $0.3c$. Each measures the speed of a radio signal from the other as $c$, not $1.8c$ or $1.3c$.
- The second postulate is consistent with Maxwell's $c = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_0\varepsilon_0}}$, which contains only constants that do not depend on the frame of reference, so $c$ in free space is the same in every frame.

## Time Dilation

**Time dilation:** a clock moving relative to an observer is measured to run slow, so the time interval it records is shorter than the observer measures.

**Proper time** $t_0$ is the time interval between two events measured by an observer at rest relative to them, so both events occur at the same position.

$$t = \frac{t_0}{\sqrt{1 - \frac{v^2}{c^2}}} = \gamma t_0$$

where $t$ is the interval measured by an observer moving at speed $v$ relative to the clock, and the **gamma factor** is

$$\gamma = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - \frac{v^2}{c^2}}}$$

$\gamma \geq 1$. It equals 1 when $v = 0$ and tends to infinity as $v \to c$.

**The light clock:** a pulse of light bounces between two mirrors, and each round trip is one tick. An observer moving relative to the clock sees the light follow a **longer diagonal path** at the **same speed** $c$, by the second postulate, so each tick takes longer.

Two observers moving at high speed relative to each other each measure the **other's** clock running slow. Neither frame is special.

**Inline example:** a clock moving at $0.6c$ records 8.0 s. A stationary observer measures $t = \frac{8.0}{\sqrt{1 - 0.6^2}} = \frac{8.0}{0.8} = 10$ s.

## Length Contraction

**Length contraction:** an object moving relative to an observer is measured to be shorter than its proper length.

**Proper length** $l_0$ is the length of an object measured by an observer at rest relative to it.

$$l = l_0\sqrt{1 - \frac{v^2}{c^2}} = \frac{l_0}{\gamma}$$

- Length contraction occurs **only along the direction of relative motion**.
- To measure the length of a moving object, an observer records the positions of both ends **at the same time** in their own frame.

**Inline example:** a rod of proper length 5.0 m moving at $0.8c$ is measured as $l = 5.0\sqrt{1 - 0.8^2} = 5.0 \times 0.6 = 3.0$ m.

## Evidence from Muon Decay

**Muons** are produced in the upper atmosphere, from the decay of pions produced by cosmic rays. They are unstable, with a half-life of about 1.5 μs in their own rest frame.

**Newtonian prediction:** muons travel 10 km at $0.98c$, taking $t = \frac{1.0 \times 10^4}{0.98 \times 3.0 \times 10^8} = 3.4 \times 10^{-5}$ s, about **23 half-lives**. Only about $\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{23}$, roughly one in ten million, would reach the ground.

**Observation:** muon count rates measured at high altitude and at ground level show that **far more** muons reach the ground than expected without time dilation.

At $0.98c$, $\gamma = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - 0.98^2}} = 5.0$.

| Frame | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Earth's frame** | The muons' half-life is **dilated** by $\gamma$, to about 7.5 μs, so only about 4.5 half-lives pass during the journey |
| **Muons' frame** | The distance to the ground is **length-contracted** by $\gamma$, to about 2.0 km, so the journey takes about 6.8 μs, again about 4.5 half-lives |

Both frames predict that about $\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{4.5}$, around 4%, of the muons reach the ground, which matches observation.

- The **proper time** of the half-life is measured in the **muon's rest frame**.
- The **proper length** of the journey is measured in the **Earth's frame**.

## Mass and Energy

### Relativistic Mass

**Rest mass** $m_0$ is the mass of an object measured by an observer at rest relative to it. The relativistic mass of an object moving at speed $v$ is

$$m = \frac{m_0}{\sqrt{1 - \frac{v^2}{c^2}}} = \gamma m_0$$

**Graph of mass against speed:** close to $m_0$ at low speeds, then rising steeply towards infinity as $v \to c$.

### Relativistic Energy

$E = mc^2$ gives the **total energy** of an object moving at speed $v$:

$$E = mc^2 = \frac{m_0c^2}{\sqrt{1 - \frac{v^2}{c^2}}}$$

The **rest energy** is $E_0 = m_0c^2$. The **kinetic energy** is the total energy minus the rest energy:

$$E_k = mc^2 - m_0c^2 = (\gamma - 1)m_0c^2$$

$\frac{1}{2}mv^2$ with the relativistic mass is **wrong** for relativistic kinetic energy, because $mc^2 - m_0c^2$ does not equal $\frac{1}{2}mv^2$.

**Inline example:** a particle with rest energy 938 MeV moving at $0.6c$ has total energy $E = \frac{938}{\sqrt{1 - 0.6^2}} = \frac{938}{0.8} \approx 1170$ MeV.

**Inline example:** an electron with rest energy 0.511 MeV and $\gamma = 3.0$ has kinetic energy $E_k = (3.0 - 1) \times 0.511 = 1.02$ MeV.

### Kinetic Energy and Speed

**Graph of kinetic energy against speed:** the relativistic and Newtonian curves are almost equal at low speeds. At high speeds, relativistic $E_k$ is greater and rises towards infinity as $v \to c$, while the Newtonian curve stays finite at $c$.

- No object with mass can be accelerated to the speed of light, because it would need an **infinite** amount of energy.
- Relativistic kinetic energy must be used when the speed is a **significant fraction** of $c$.
- Electrons passing through several equal accelerating pds gain the same kinetic energy, $eV$, at each stage, but the gain in **speed** is far smaller in the last stage as the speed approaches $c$.

## Bertozzi's Experiment

Bertozzi's experiment provided **direct evidence** for the relativistic variation of kinetic energy with speed.

**Method:**

1. Electrons are accelerated from rest through different potential differences, giving them different kinetic energies $E_k = eV$.
2. After leaving the electric field, they travel at **constant speed** through an evacuated tube, because no forces act on them.
3. Their flight is timed between two signal points a known distance apart, using an oscilloscope. Speed = distance / time.
4. Plot **speed squared** ($v^2$) against **kinetic energy**.

**Checking the kinetic energy independently:** the electrons hit an aluminium target. The energy it gained was measured from its temperature rise using $\Delta E = mc\Delta\theta$. The number of electrons hitting it was the total charge collected divided by the electron charge, $n = \frac{Q}{e}$. Dividing the energy by $n$ gave the kinetic energy per electron, which agreed with $eV$.

**Inline example:** a target that collects $3.2\ \mu\text{C}$ of charge has been hit by $n = \frac{3.2 \times 10^{-6}}{1.6 \times 10^{-19}} = 2.0 \times 10^{13}$ electrons.

**Results:**

| Prediction | Graph of $v^2$ against $E_k$ |
|---|---|
| Newtonian | Straight line through the origin, with $v^2$ exceeding $c^2$ at high energies |
| Relativistic | $v^2$ levels off, approaching $c^2$ |

Bertozzi's results supported the **relativistic** prediction: electron speeds approached but never exceeded $c$.
